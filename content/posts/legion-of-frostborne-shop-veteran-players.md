## Legion’s Vender Guide: The Best Ways to Spend Cold Silver

The **Legion’s Vender** is one of the most important long-term progression shops in Infinity Kingdom. Cold Silver arrives in a fairly large bundle at the end of a successful KvK, but the numbers are deceptive: unlocking a single Ability Stone skill requires 60 stones at 800 Cold Silver each, bringing the total cost to **48,000 Cold Silver**. Once you begin buying complete skills, even a strong season’s earnings disappear very quickly.

This is why Cold Silver should be planned across several KvK seasons rather than treated like an ordinary event currency. For most players, the vendor is first and foremost a way to expand their skill collection, both through the Ability Stones sold directly in the shop and through unwanted stones that can be recycled into Cinders for use in the Cinder Shop. Later, once the important skills are covered, the same currency becomes one of the best ways to work on harder-to-obtain account upgrades such as the Wind Dragon.

There are resources, speedups, artifact materials and cosmetics in the shop as well, but availability does not necessarily make something a good purchase. Cold Silver accumulates too slowly to spend without a plan, and every ordinary item bought today delays an exclusive skill or a meaningful amount of Wind Dragon progression later. The purpose of this guide is therefore not simply to list what the vendor contains, but to establish a sensible purchase order for both developing accounts and veterans who have already collected most of what they need.

## Ability Stones Should Be Your First Priority

For newer players, the first priority is straightforward: work towards unlocking all the skills available in Legion’s Vender. A complete skill requires 60 Ability Stones, with each stone costing 800 Cold Silver. That brings the total cost of one skill to: **60 Ability Stones × 800 Cold Silver = 48,000 Cold Silver**

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4-1024x529.png)

At the upper end of KvK rewards, a highly active player in a successful faction can earn enough to unlock approximately one complete skill per season. Reaching that amount is not guaranteed simply because your faction wins, however. Your personal contribution, alliance reputation, ranking and control of important objectives all affect the final amount.

With the current number of skills available, completing the entire collection represents roughly twelve excellent KvK seasons. For most players, it will take longer, which is why these skills should be treated as a long-term project rather than something you expect to finish quickly.

You do not necessarily need to buy the skills in a fixed order. Start with whichever skill offers the greatest improvement to your main march, then continue working through the remaining options. The important point is that you should not interrupt this progression to buy resources or consumables that can easily be obtained elsewhere.

Ability Stone skills provide permanent account value. Speed-ups and resources disappear as soon as they are used, while a skill can remain relevant across different marches and metas for years. That difference is what makes the skills considerably more valuable than almost everything else in the shop.

## What Should Veteran Players Buy?

Once you have unlocked all the KvK Ability Stone skills, the shop becomes much more flexible. At that stage, the best remaining progression purchases are generally **Wind Dragon Crystals** and **Dragon Essence**.

Wind Dragon Crystals are the obvious choice if you are still developing the Wind Dragon or expect to use it in one of your marches. Dragon Essence is a broader investment in dragon progression and remains useful for accounts that still have dragons or dragon talents left to develop.

Both options provide lasting account progression and are relatively difficult to accumulate in large quantities. That makes them considerably better uses of Cold Silver than ordinary resources, speed-ups or other items you can farm through regular gameplay.

## Wind Dragon Crystals: The Best Long-Term Purchase After Skills

Once the important skills have been secured, **Wind Dragon Crystals should become one of your main priorities in the Legion’s Vender**. The Wind Dragon is not simply a niche alternative for players running a traditional Wind march. It is one of the strongest high-end dragons to have available, with excellent base attributes and powerful specializations that can significantly increase the Ultimate Damage of the march using it.

There are also far more places to use the Wind Dragon than may initially be obvious. An Alexander–Hannibal formation is one good example, particularly when Alexander is not being used in your primary march. Many developed accounts now run a dual-tank frontline with the Earth Dragon on their main team, leaving the Wind Dragon available for an aggressive physical or hybrid secondary march. The extra offensive attributes and Ultimate Damage can produce excellent results in those formations, especially when the Immortals already have enough protection from their own skills and supports.

Hybrid marches open up even more possibilities because the Wind Dragon does not need to be restricted to four Wind Immortals. I am currently working on a Holy hybrid that uses some interesting interactions involving Dodge and Ultimate Damage, and this is only one of several directions in which the dragon can be taken. Its combination of superior attributes and offensive specializations makes it useful across a wide range of physical, hybrid and secondary formations. Even if you do not have an immediate place for it, developing the Wind Dragon gives you another genuinely strong option as your marches and the wider meta continue to change.

The problem is availability. Wind Dragon Crystals are extremely difficult to accumulate through normal gameplay, particularly in the quantities required to develop the dragon’s talents and complete its later progression. The KvK shop is one of the few places where you can collect a meaningful number of crystals every season, which makes them considerably more valuable than ordinary resources, speedups or artifact materials.

That does not mean newer players should immediately spend all their Cold Silver on the Wind Dragon. **Skills still come first**, including both the Ability Stones sold directly in the KvK shop and important skills from the Cinder Shop. KvK Ability Stones can be recycled into Cinders, allowing Cold Silver to help you obtain skills that are not directly available from the Legion’s Vender. If you do not need a skill such as Snipe, for example, you can buy its stones, sell them for Cinders and use those Cinders toward another skill that your marches actually need.

This makes the correct priority slightly broader than simply buying every KvK skill. First secure the useful skills available directly from the vendor, then consider whether unwanted KvK stones can help you complete your Cinder Shop collection. Only when your important skill needs are covered should Wind Dragon Crystals become the main target for your Cold Silver. Skills will normally have the largest immediate effect on your account, but once that stage is finished, the Wind Dragon offers one of the best long-term investments available in the entire shop.

## What Should You Avoid Buying?

There are several items I would almost never recommend purchasing with Cold Silver:

- Philosopher’s Stones
- Teleports
- Artifact materials
- Speed-ups
- Resources

None of these items are useless. The problem is that Infinity Kingdom already gives us many other ways to obtain them through events, shops, bundles, daily activities and ordinary farming. Cold Silver, by comparison, is earned through a limited seasonal event and accumulates very slowly.

Using Cold Silver to buy resources or speed-ups is effectively exchanging months of permanent skill progression for a small amount of temporary convenience. It may help you finish something today, but it will delay an exclusive skill that could strengthen your account permanently.

Artifact materials are slightly more tempting because artifact development can be painfully slow. Even so, the quantities available do not normally justify sacrificing progress towards Ability Stones or dragon materials. Unless your account has already exhausted every better option, I would leave them alone.

Cosmetics belong in a different category. Chat boxes, frames and nameplates do not provide meaningful combat value, but efficiency is not the only reason people play the game. If you find a design you genuinely like and would enjoy using, there is nothing wrong with buying it. Just understand that you are choosing appearance over progression and make the decision accordingly.

## What If You Already Have Everything?

Older accounts may eventually reach a point where all the KvK skills are unlocked, the relevant dragons are finished and none of the remaining shop items provide a meaningful upgrade. If that describes your account, there is no reason to spend Cold Silver simply because it is available.

You can keep the currency for future shop additions or reserve it for the chests available around the end of KvK. Those chests can provide access to far more interesting rewards, including valuable Holy and Shadow Immortal fragments, and Cold Silver can be used in place of Gems when opening them.

This is one of the few situations in Infinity Kingdom where refusing to buy anything may be the most efficient choice. Spending a rare currency on items you do not need does not improve your account; it only removes your ability to take advantage of something better later.

## Final Recommendation

For most players, Legion’s Vender should be viewed as an Ability Stone shop first and everything else second. Buy the skill that benefits your main march, complete it, and then begin working towards the next one. Continue until the entire KvK skill collection is unlocked, even if that process takes many seasons.

Once the skills are finished, move on to Wind Dragon Crystals or Dragon Essence according to your account’s needs. Cosmetics are perfectly reasonable if you enjoy them, but common resources, teleports, speed-ups, Philosopher’s Stones and artifact materials offer poor value for such a limited currency.

Cold Silver rewards patience. The players who gain the most from it are rarely the ones who spend it fastest, but the ones who consistently direct it towards progression that will still matter several KvK seasons from now.
