Patch 2.9.9 introduced a new **Immortal Demonstration** feature that allows players to watch selected Immortals fight as part of a complete formation.

At first glance, this is clearly a marketing feature. The game places the newest Immortal inside a carefully selected march and demonstrates that formation defeating an older meta setup. The purpose is not difficult to understand: show players the new Immortal winning, create excitement around the release, and encourage people to obtain it.

That does not make the feature useless, however. Holy, Shadow and especially Chaos Immortals can be extremely expensive to unlock and develop. Being able to watch them perform in a proper battle before investing provides information that a skill description alone cannot offer.

## How to Access the Battle Simulation

Open the Immortal interface and select an Immortal that supports the new demonstration feature. Patch 2.9.9 initially introduced it alongside newer Immortals such as Horus, although more Immortals may receive demonstrations later.

In the bottom-left corner of the Immortal screen, you will see a new **Simulation Combat** icon. Select it to open the demonstration lineup.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-35-1024x549.png)

The game will display the two formations, their Immortals, Dragons and total power before allowing you to begin the **Simulation Challenge**.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-36-1024x499.png)

Once the battle begins, it plays like a normal combat replay. This lets you observe the new Immortal’s Ultimate, skill timing, targeting, survivability and interaction with the rest of the formation.

## Why the Feature Is Useful

Immortal descriptions tell us what an ability is supposed to do, but they do not always communicate how that ability feels in a complete battle.

This is particularly relevant for Horus. His passive prevents any individual instance of incoming damage from exceeding a percentage of his maximum HP, while his active skill damages all enemies and can apply Punishment. On paper, this suggests an unusually durable back-row mage that is difficult to remove with concentrated burst damage.

The simulation allows players to see that durability in practice. You can watch how long Horus survives, how frequently he casts, how his damage develops and how his abilities interact with the recommended supporting Immortals and skills.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-32-1024x560.png)

It also gives players an opportunity to inspect a complete suggested formation. Even if you do not intend to copy it exactly, it may reveal useful combinations or help explain what role the developers expect the new Immortal to perform.

That is valuable when developing a premium Immortal can cost hundreds or even thousands of dollars. A demonstration cannot replace genuine player testing, but it is considerably better than making that investment based entirely on a written skill description.

## This Is Mostly Marketing, but It Is Still Useful

The demonstration should not be mistaken for independent balance testing.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-33-1024x465.png)

The new Immortal is placed inside a formation selected by the developers and matched against a specific opposing team. Differences in skills, positioning, Dragon development, attributes and formation power can all influence the outcome. There is also an important matchup issue hidden inside the first demonstration.

In my opinion, this initial preview battle is probably more a case of **Great Tengu countering Ares** than definitive proof that **Horus outperforms Medusa**. The new formation changes more than one Immortal, so we cannot isolate Horus and attribute the entire result to him.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-34-1024x564.png)

Anyway, that is a discussion for another guide entirely!

The wider point is that one demonstration battle cannot establish a new meta. It shows that the promoted formation can defeat the particular opponent selected for the preview. It does not tell us how either march will perform against every other popular composition.

Strong Medusa and Ares formations should still be perfectly capable of competing with Horus teams. Medusa remains an exceptionally powerful damage dealer, and a developed player formation may use different skills, artifacts, specializations and supporting Immortals from those shown in the simulation.

## What the Simulation Cannot Tell Us

The demonstration does not replace testing in Arena, Legion of Frostborne, Illusion Battlefield or real server combat.

It cannot fully answer questions such as:

- How does the formation perform against several different meta teams?
- Does Horus remain effective when the opponent uses anti-healing or sustained damage?
- How dependent is the result on Great Tengu?
- How does Horus compare with an equally developed Medusa?
- Which Tower of Knowledge skills produce the best results?
- Does the recommended formation remain strong when both sides use optimized artifacts and specializations?

Those answers require battle reports and repeated testing against multiple formations. A single favourable result should therefore be treated as a preview, not as proof that the previous meta has become obsolete.

## Final Thoughts

The Immortal Demonstration feature is promotional, but it is a welcome addition.

Players should remain sceptical of any simulation designed specifically to showcase a newly released Immortal. The formation, opponent and conditions have all been selected to present that Immortal favourably. Winning the demonstration does not automatically make the new hero mandatory or prove that the featured lineup is the strongest march in the game.

Even so, the feature lets us watch expensive Immortals interact with a complete team before deciding whether to build them. We can examine their animations, ability timing, survivability and intended synergies without first making a major financial commitment.

Use the simulation as an introduction to the Immortal, not as a final verdict on the meta. It is a useful preview and a clever piece of marketing, but the real test will always begin when players start placing Horus into their own formations.
