## Introduction

The **Alliance Contribution Rating** in Contention of Relics is one of those Infinity Kingdom statistics that looks more straightforward than it really is.

Open the alliance ranking and every member has a number beside their name. Naturally, players tend to read the list as a performance ranking: first place contributed the most, second place contributed the second most, and so on. That interpretation needs some context - especially for players managing an alliance and who are deciding what players to award the big CoR reward bundles!

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-148-1024x443.png)

Contribution Rating is useful for measuring **activity during CoR**, but different activities can generate contribution points while having very different effects on the alliance's actual objectives. One of the clearest examples is **Chaos Beast farming**.

A player can accumulate substantial Contribution Rating by repeatedly killing Chaos Beasts for personal Valor Marks. Meanwhile, another player may spend much of CoR gathering Energy Nodes, attending Relic sieges, fighting around objectives, or helping with the World Heart.

The important principle is:

> **Alliance Contribution Rating measures contribution points earned. It should not automatically be treated as a ranking of who contributed most toward winning CoR.**

Understanding that distinction is particularly important for R4 and R5 players deciding how alliance rewards should be distributed.

---

## What Is Alliance Contribution Rating?

During Contention of Relics, participating players accumulate **Contribution Rating** from various event activities.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-147-1024x305.png)

The resulting ranking is visible from the alliance interface and provides an easy overview of member activity. There is nothing inherently wrong with using this ranking.

A player sitting at 10,000+ Contribution Rating has clearly been doing CoR activities. Someone sitting at zero clearly has not.

The problem appears when we try to infer **what kind of contribution produced those points**.

Not every contribution point represents the same strategic value.

---

## Chaos Beasts Generate a Lot of Contribution Rating

Chaos Beasts provide the easiest example.

Based on current in-game testing, defeating Chaos Beasts awards:

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-146-1024x141.png)

Level 3 farming adds up particularly quickly.

Ten kills produce **1,590 Contribution Rating**.

Twenty produce **3,180**.

Thirty produce **4,770**.

Forty produce **6,360**.

At 159 points per kill, roughly 76 Level 3 Chaos Beast kills would generate just over **12,000 Contribution Rating**.

This does **not** mean that someone with 12,000 rating killed 76 Chaos Beasts. Contribution Rating comes from multiple CoR activities.

It demonstrates why the final number cannot tell us how that player spent their time.

---

## Why Players Farm Chaos Beasts

There is a perfectly good reason to kill them: **Valor Marks**.

Valor Marks are used to purchase items from the CoR Mystery Shop. The game identifies several ways of earning them, including defeating Chaos Beasts, capturing Energy Nodes, fighting enemy Lords, and rewards connected to Relics and the World Heart.

For individual progression, Chaos Beast farming is therefore useful.

If the battlefield is quiet and your alliance does not need your march somewhere else, there is little reason to leave it idle. Farming Chaos Beasts turns that downtime into additional Valor Marks.

The distinction is between **personal value** and **alliance value**.

A Chaos Beast farmer receives personal Valor Marks while also generating Contribution Rating.

An Energy Node player is helping generate Energy Cubes.

A siege player may be spending troops and healing speedups so the alliance can capture or retain a Relic.

All three can increase their Contribution Rating, but their effect on the wider CoR campaign is not identical.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-145-1024x523.png)

## Contribution Rating Is Best Treated as an Activity Indicator

This is how I have found the ranking most useful in practice.

**Contribution Rating answers:**
*"Has this player been active in CoR?"*

It is much worse at answering:
*"How important was this player to our alliance's CoR performance?"*

At the extremes, the leaderboard remains informative. A player with zero rating has obviously done little or nothing that generates contribution. A player with a very high score has clearly invested time into event activities. The ambiguity lies between active players.

Suppose Player A has **8,000 Contribution Rating** and Player B has **5,000**.

The ranking tells us that Player A accumulated 3,000 more contribution points. It does not tell us that Player A was 60% more useful to the alliance.

Player A may have spent substantial time farming Chaos Beasts. Player B may have been occupying Energy Nodes and immediately moving whenever leadership called for a siege.

Without knowing the activities behind the numbers, the comparison is incomplete.

---

## Energy Cubes Are the More Important Strategic Context

The reason this distinction matters is that **Contention of Relics is ultimately decided around Energy Cubes and objective control**.

The in-game rules state that kingdoms are ranked according to the Energy Cubes they obtain during CoR. Relics, the World Heart, and Energy Nodes are therefore not merely alternative ways to increase a personal statistic; they feed into the wider competition.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-149-1024x407.png)

Energy Cubes also matter at the alliance level because reaching specific thresholds produces enormous pools of Valor Marks.

The current thresholds shown in-game are:

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-150-1024x388.png)

These numbers provide much better context for evaluating certain kinds of participation.

A player who helps push an alliance across the **200,000 Energy Cube threshold**, for example, is helping move its Valor Mark pool from 1.5 million to **2.2 million**.

Reaching 300,000 increases the pool to **3.6 million Valor Marks**.

That contribution benefits the alliance as a whole.

---

## Contribution Rating and Reward Distribution

Contribution Rating is useful, but I would never distribute CoR rewards purely from the leaderboard. It tells you that someone was active; it does not tell you how useful that activity was to the alliance.

When distributing Valor Marks, I care more about who **showed up for Relic and World Heart sieges, collected Energy Nodes, completed alliance objectives, and responded when help was needed**. Roles matter too. A strong PvP player may spend most of CoR clearing enemies rather than gathering, while a smaller account can contribute far more by consistently collecting nodes. Both are doing useful work.

Chaos Beasts are where the leaderboard gets deceptive. Farming them is perfectly worthwhile during downtime because you earn personal Valor Marks and Contribution Rating at the same time. However, someone farming Level 3 Beasts for hours can end up above a player who spent the event gathering cubes and burning troops at sieges. I would not automatically give the Beast farmer a larger share of the alliance Valor pool just because the final number is higher. They have already been earning personal Valor from that farming, while the other player was helping create the alliance reward being distributed.

This is also why I dislike rigid systems where Rank 1 gets the most, Rank 2 gets the second-most, and so on. Players quickly figure out what produces Contribution Rating efficiently and optimize around it. You can unintentionally encourage people to farm Beasts while the alliance actually needs marches on nodes or objectives. **Reward the activity you want people to repeat next CoR.**

You do not need a spreadsheet tracking every march to accomplish that. R4s and R5s generally know who attended sieges, who consistently gathered nodes, and who answered calls. Use Contribution Rating as supporting information rather than the final verdict, and make the reward criteria clear before CoR starts.

None of this means players should stop farming Chaos Beasts. I farm them whenever objectives are covered and there is nothing more useful for my march to be doing. The problem is only when personal Valor farming takes priority over alliance objectives.

There is another layer to all of this because the alliance Valor rewards scale with Energy Cubes: **400,000 Valor at 10,000 cubes, 900,000 at 50,000, 1.5 million at 100,000, 2.2 million at 200,000, and 3.6 million at 300,000**. An organized kingdom can actually exploit those thresholds by splitting active players between farm alliances, pushing several alliances through efficient cube milestones, banking the Valor, and moving players around for distribution.

That gets considerably more complicated, though. **It is probably a guide of its own.** Stay tuned!

---

## Conclusion

Contribution Rating is best treated as a rough measure of CoR activity, not a final judgment of who contributed the most. Chaos Beasts alone can generate thousands of points while mainly rewarding the player with personal Valor Marks, so the number needs context.

For alliance rewards, look at what players actually did: **who collected nodes, attended sieges, completed alliance objectives, fought when needed, and helped generate the Energy Cubes behind the alliance's Valor pool**.

And keep farming Chaos Beasts. They are good value whenever there is downtime. Just don't mistake a high Contribution Rating from Beast farming for the same thing as helping the alliance win CoR.

**Use the leaderboard as a reference, not the reward sheet.**
