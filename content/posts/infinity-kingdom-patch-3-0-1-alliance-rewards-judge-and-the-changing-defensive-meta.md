Alliance War Points create another route to Chaos, Holy and Shadow Immortal fragments, while Operation Frontline gives alliances convoy escorts and interceptions to organize. On the battlefield, Judge introduces a new source of repeated magical damage, Horus becomes harder to counter through dispels, Charles gains faster energy regeneration, and Yi Sun-Shin trades some damage reduction for additional Defense.

The result is a patch with potential benefits for everyday progression and some significant questions for established formations. Here is what changes, why it matters and what to consider before adjusting your builds.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153241-v2xy31.png)

## Alliance War Points: A New Route to Valuable Rewards

Alliance War Points can be earned directly through alliance events or distributed by alliance leadership from event rewards. They are spent in the Alliance Shop, whose announced rewards include **Chaos Immortal fragments, Holy and Shadow Immortal fragments, and Ability Stones**.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153329-7s3di9.png)

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153356-mr5ad8.png)

That is an important addition for players developing expensive Immortals over time. Another acquisition route can make progress less dependent on individual spending events, provided the amount earned and the shop’s prices make the rewards realistically accessible.

However, the presence of Chaos fragments in a shop does not establish how quickly players can develop an Immortal. Before judging its value, check the live prices, purchase limits, available selections and your own War Point income. A small regular supply can still be worthwhile, but it should be evaluated as part of a longer development plan.

The distribution system also gives alliance leadership more responsibility. Clear expectations about participation and reward allocation will help members understand what they can earn and how to contribute.

## Operation Frontline: Escorts, Interceptions and Alliance Coordination

Operation Frontline is the new convoy event accessed through the alliance interface. Alliances dispatch transports and defend their supplies, while other alliances can organize interception rallies.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153316-738bef.png)

Participation requires **Alliance Level 2 and Castle Level 10**. Only R5 and R6 members can create convoys, making scheduling an alliance responsibility.

### Guardians and Transporters Have Different Roles

Guardians take the combat role and can be challenged by interception squads. Their rewards depend on convoy type and the supplies remaining, with the relevant War Points distributed by alliance leadership.

Transporters participate without being challenged in combat and receive a smaller amount of War Points upon successful delivery. This gives players a way to participate without taking the same defensive role as the alliance’s stronger accounts.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153027-5dibj8.png)

Successful interceptions award War Points to participating members. Failed interceptions provide no reward, so use your daily attempt on a target your group has a realistic chance of defeating.

### Make Participation Work for Your Alliance

The practical challenge is giving members a reasonable opportunity to join while protecting valuable deliveries. Publish convoy times, account for different time zones and explain how Guardian rewards will be distributed.

Strong defenders can protect the convoy, while Transporter opportunities give less-developed accounts a useful participation route. Repeatedly filling every lineup with the same small group limits the wider benefit of the system.

For individual players, learn your alliance’s schedule and use the participation opportunities available to you. For leadership, treat the event as a recurring progression activity that needs organization, rather than assuming the daily convoy allowance will manage itself.

## Judge: A New Legendary Magical Damage Skill

Judge is a Legendary passive obtained through the **Legendary Season Wish Shop**. It deals magical damage to all enemies every six seconds, with **50% increased damage against targets affected by Punishment**.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153429-zdbwf6.png)

At maximum level, its base damage rate is 500%. The Punishment bonus raises that coefficient to an effective 750% against affected targets, before other damage modifiers and defenses are considered.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153049-c59320.png)

This makes Horus an obvious partner because his ultimate applies Punishment. However, **Judge does not require Horus to be its bearer**. Its bonus checks the enemy’s status, so another magical attacker in a formation containing Horus can also be considered.

The important decision is which Immortal makes the best use of the skill and what they must give up to equip it. Putting Judge on Horus consumes one of his slots; assigning it elsewhere may preserve his existing setup while adding another source of damage.

Also consider timing. Judge attacks on a six-second interval, while Punishment lasts six seconds after being applied. Its bonus depends on which targets are affected when each hit occurs. Strong synergy on paper still needs to translate into useful damage during the actual battle.

## Horus: Less Damage Amplification, More Reliable Punishment

Horus’s Punishment can no longer be dispelled. In exchange, its maximum damage-taken increase falls from **15% to 10%**. His passive damage cap and ultimate’s base damage are unchanged in these notes.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153059-h0a7nq.png)

Against opponents who previously removed Punishment, this improves the reliability of Horus’s contribution. The effect can remain for its duration, helping both the formation’s damage and Judge’s conditional bonus.

Against opponents who were not removing it, the lower amplification is a genuine reduction. Whether the overall change benefits Horus therefore depends partly on the opponents he faces.

**Undispellable also does not mean permanently active.** Punishment still has a 60% application chance and a limited duration. Horus needs to cast, successfully apply it and maintain useful coverage through the battle.

## Yi Sun-Shin: Damage Reduction and Defense Are Different

Yi Sun-Shin’s Guard of Devotion receives the patch’s most important defensive tradeoff. At maximum level, its damage reduction falls from **30% to 20%**, while the skill adds **20% Physical and Magical Defense** during its protection window.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153114-khbvzb.png)

The new percentages cannot simply be added together. Defense and damage reduction affect combat differently, so 20% Defense does not automatically replace the lost 10 percentage points of damage reduction.

### How Large Is the Damage-Reduction Loss?

Looking only at that component, an incoming damage amount of 100 becomes 70 under the old 30% reduction and 80 under the new 20% reduction. That is approximately **14.3% more damage received before accounting for the newly added Defense**.

For the new Defense to offset that difference in this simplified comparison, it would need to reduce the incoming amount before this damage-reduction step by 12.5%.

This calculation illustrates the tradeoff; it is not a prediction of the final battle result. The actual outcome depends on the damage source, attack and defense interactions, and other active effects.

For players already using YSS, the sensible response is to evaluate familiar matchups before replacing him. Check whether the formation survives the same pressure, how well the protection window covers important enemy attacks, and whether another defensive Immortal produces better results.

## Charles the Great: A Meaningful Energy Regeneration Buff

Charles’s base Energy Regen increases from **64 to 80 points per second**, a 25% improvement. That gives him a stronger foundation for reaching his ultimate and reapplying Terra Shield.

Looking at regeneration alone, the time needed to accumulate a fixed amount of energy falls by 20%. Actual casting intervals will also reflect other energy sources, energy reduction and control effects.

The value of the buff depends on whether earlier shields arrive at useful moments. A shield before a major enemy burst can change the result, while shields that are quickly stripped or overwhelmed may provide less benefit.

Charles is therefore worth revisiting if you already have him developed, particularly alongside the changes to YSS. The patch does not establish that he becomes the better choice in every formation.

## Activity Gifts and Additional Alliance Rewards

Alliance Gifts now include **Activity Gifts** alongside the recharge-based gifts, which are renamed **Golden Gifts**. Activity Gifts can come from Daily Missions, Infernal Assault and Alliance Supply Convoys.

Infernal Assault also adds Alliance War Points and Alliance Activity Chests to its rewards. Together, these changes give regular alliance participation more progression value and make consistent activity relevant to the rewards shared within the group.

Gilded Club receives new Active Quests, with Chaos Immortal Selection Chests among the Fortune Wheel rewards mentioned in the notes. Their presence alone does not establish good value; check the actual milestones and coin requirements before planning around them.

## Quality-of-Life Improvements

Several smaller changes reduce repetitive actions and simplify event management.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261121-153130-f49kp5.png)



When managing Illusion Battlefield participants, remember that using Select All again overrides manual deselections. Complete the bulk selection first, then make individual changes.

## Skill Description Corrections Are Not Additional Balance Changes

The developers also clarified that the revised English skill descriptions are **text-only corrections**. They do not change skill effects, values or battle behavior.

Those corrections include clarifications about whose damage triggers an effect, which types of skills receive a trigger-probability bonus, and how certain defensive effects are described.

Keep these separate from the explicit YSS, Horus and Charles adjustments. A clearer tooltip may change how we understand an existing mechanic without changing what happens in battle.

## What Should Players Focus On?

For developing accounts, the alliance systems are the most useful starting point. Learn the convoy schedule, participate in events that award War Points and compare the shop’s options with your long-term Immortal plans.

For players using YSS or Charles, revisit the defensive performance of your current formation before committing resources to a replacement. The patch changes their relative strengths, but the right choice still depends on the team and opponents.

For Legendary Season players considering Judge, identify its intended bearer and the skill it would replace. Judge’s interaction with Punishment is clear, while its best placement and practical advantage over an existing setup require comparison.

## Final Thoughts

Patch 3.0.1 gives alliances a more direct role in player progression while changing several important combat decisions. War Points and convoy rewards create opportunities for steady development, provided members can participate and the shop economy supports meaningful progress.

The combat changes reward a closer look at individual formations. Horus gains more reliable Punishment, Judge adds a new damage option, Charles reaches his shields faster, and YSS exchanges part of his damage reduction for Defense.

Use those confirmed changes to decide what to test. They provide good reasons to revisit builds, without making every early verdict or proposed replacement an established upgrade.
