Infinity Kingdom is participating in Gamescom 2026 alongside AppGallery, with the event running in Cologne, Germany, from **August 26–30**. The AppGallery/GameCenter booth is located in **Hall 7, Booth B.031**, where Infinity Kingdom is one of the featured games.

## Infinity Kingdom Gamescom 2026 Special Packs: Value Analysis and Buying Guide

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-139-1024x712.png)

Alongside the Gamescom appearance, several limited **Gamescom Special Packs** have appeared through the external shop. At first glance, the advertised discounts are substantial: 95%, 90%, 85%, 66%, and 48% off.

Those percentages are not particularly useful when deciding what to buy, though. What matters is how difficult the included resources normally are to obtain and whether a cheaper route already exists.

There are five main Gamescom packs:

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-140-1024x231.png)

The three standard $0.99, $1.99, and $2.99 vouchers visible alongside them are regular voucher purchases rather than the main Gamescom bundles. GTarcade currently lists these vouchers separately with daily purchase limits.

## Gamescom Special Pack 1 – Artifact Schematic Fragments

**Price:** $4.99
**Purchase limit:** 5
**Reward:** 60 Artifact Schematic Fragment Selecting Chests
**Advertised discount:** 95%

The first pack is one of the better offers, although its value depends heavily on where your account is in artifact progression.

The selection chests contain fragments for specific exclusive artifacts. Before buying anything, check the chests already sitting in your inventory and confirm that the artifact you actually need is included.

At the full five-purchase limit, you are looking at: **$24.95 → 300 schematic fragments**

That is a meaningful amount of targeted artifact progression.

Compared with buying similar progression through events such as Stellar Compass, the Gamescom offer is attractive. However, artifact fragments are only valuable when they move an artifact toward a meaningful breakpoint.

For example, if you are actively finishing an artifact for an Immortal such as **Khan or Alex**, 60 targeted fragments for $4.99 can remove a substantial amount of waiting. Buying all five packs can provide roughly a third of the fragments required to fully develop an artifact.

If you have no immediate artifact target, there is much less reason to stockpile the chests simply because the shop says "95% off."

### Player Perspective

I would classify this as a **conditional buy rather than a universal buy**.

For an account already committed to finishing a specific premium artifact, the value is quite good. For an account still deciding which march or Immortal deserves the investment, keeping the money available for a more targeted event is perfectly reasonable.

The resource itself is valuable. The question is whether you need it now.

## Gamescom Special Pack 2 – Theia's Roulette Tickets

**Price:** $9.99
**Purchase limit:** 5
**Reward:** 32 Theia's Roulette Raffle Tickets
**Advertised discount:** 90%

This is the standout Gamescom pack.

A normal comparable $10 purchase provides approximately **20 roulette tickets**. The Gamescom version provides **32**, meaning you receive 12 additional tickets at essentially the same spending level.

That works out to: **60% more tickets for the same approximate price.**

Buying all five gives: **$49.95 → 160 Roulette Tickets**

For players actively progressing the **Holy Dragon**, this is easily the strongest purchase among the Gamescom offers.

It can also make sense for Shadow Dragon progression, although between the two I would generally prioritize Holy Dragon development unless the account has a specific reason to invest into Shadow.

The important distinction from Pack 1 is that the value proposition does not require nearly as much justification. If Theia's Roulette is already part of your planned dragon progression, you are essentially buying a resource you intended to acquire anyway at a substantially improved rate.

### Player Perspective

If I were spending selectively during this promotion, **Pack 2 would be my first purchase**.

Pack 1 solves an artifact progression problem. Pack 2 simply gives significantly more of an already desirable premium resource for the money.

That makes it the cleanest value proposition of the five.

## Gamescom Special Pack 3 – Master's Insights

**Price:** $19.99
**Purchase limit:** 5
**Reward:** 80 Master's Insights
**Advertised discount:** 85%

This one looks considerably better on the shop page than it does after comparing alternative sources.

You receive: **$19.99 → 80 Master's Insights**

The problem is not that Master's Insights are unimportant. The problem is that Infinity Kingdom already provides much more efficient long-term ways to obtain them.

The **30-Day Elite Campaign Supplies** in Kingdom Supplies costs around $10. Between the additional Elite World attempts and the daily Master's Insights provided through the supply rewards, an active player completing the relevant daily content can obtain roughly **930 Master's Insights across the month**.

The Gamescom comparison therefore becomes difficult to justify:

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-141-1024x111.png)

The Gamescom pack buys immediacy. It does not buy efficiency.

### Player Perspective

This is a **hard pass for most accounts**.

There are circumstances where somebody might desperately need Master's Insights immediately, but you are paying an enormous premium to avoid waiting.

For normal progression, I would rather buy the monthly supply and let the resource accumulate naturally.

## Gamescom Special Pack 4 – City of Glory

**Price:** $49.99
**Purchase limit:** 1
**Main rewards:** City of Glory Castle Skin + 30 Eternal Dragon Soul Chests
**Advertised discount:** 66%

This pack requires separating cosmetic value from progression value.

The **City of Glory** castle skin provides useful bonuses, including:

- **Troop HP +2%**
- **5% Honor when attacking enemies**

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-142-1024x619.png)

Neither effect is useless. Troop HP is broadly applicable, while additional Honor has obvious value for players who participate heavily in PvP. The problem is opportunity cost.

The castle itself has previously appeared around this spending tier, including during anniversary content. More importantly, a roughly $50 **Monthly Festival Pass** can provide a castle skin alongside additional rewards such as Immortal Costume materials, Gems, and event currencies. That gives you considerably more progression surrounding the cosmetic purchase.

The Gamescom pack attempts to compensate with **30 Eternal Dragon Soul Chests**, representing **300 Holy or Shadow Dragon Crystals** depending on selection.

If your real objective is dragon development, the discounted Theia's Roulette tickets provide a much cleaner way to spend toward that goal.

There is another long-term consideration: **City of Glory currently lacks the upgrade potential available to some other castle skins**. A fixed 2% Troop HP bonus is useful, but the abundance of more powerful upgradeable skins can eventually outscale it. ***Check the comparison for Combat Buffs from Castles here.***

### Player Perspective

I would buy Pack 4 primarily because I specifically wanted **City of Glory**, not because I wanted efficient account progression.

That distinction matters.

For collectors, the castle may justify the $49.99 price on its own. For a player evaluating every dollar according to combat progression, Pack 2 is considerably more compelling.

**Verdict: skip unless the castle skin itself is the objective.**

## Gamescom Special Pack 5 – Tokugawa and Hammurabi

**Price:** $99.99
**Purchase limit:** 1 per day
**Main rewards:**

- 60 Tokugawa fragments
- 60 Hammurabi fragments
- 60 random Enlightening Immortal fragments
- 60 Dust

**Advertised discount:** 48%

This is where the Gamescom promotion falls apart.

Tokugawa and Hammurabi have some scarcity because they are not standard Hall of Immortals acquisitions. Scarcity alone, however, does not make a $100 bundle efficient.

The useful comparison is **Elemental Bond**. Through Elemental Bond, comparable quantities of these Immortal fragments can be acquired around the **$30 spending level**, with additional Dragon Crystals attached to the purchases.

That leaves a very uncomfortable question for Pack 5: What exactly accounts for the remaining ~$70?

Primarily the extra Enlightening fragments and **60 Dust**.

While dust is in high demand, sixty Dust does not bridge that value gap. There is also a particularly strange design choice here. Unlike Packs 1–4, which have event-wide purchase limits, **the $99.99 pack refreshes daily**.

So the most expensive and least efficient Gamescom offer is also the one players are allowed to repeatedly purchase.

### Player Perspective

I would avoid this pack.

It is possible to construct an account-specific scenario where someone urgently wants both featured Immortals and values every additional fragment. Even in that situation, I would compare the cost against Elemental Bond before purchasing.

Calling something "48% off" does not matter when the underlying resources can be obtained much more efficiently elsewhere.

This is easily the **weakest Gamescom pack**.

For spenders trying to maximize progression rather than simply collect everything, my order would be:

**1. Gamescom Special Pack 2 – Theia's Roulette Tickets**
Best overall value. Particularly strong for Holy Dragon progression.

**2. Gamescom Special Pack 1 – Artifact Schematic Fragments**
Very good when you are actively finishing a specific exclusive artifact.

**3. Gamescom Special Pack 4 – City of Glory**
Reasonable for collectors, but difficult to recommend purely for progression.

**4. Gamescom Special Pack 3 – Master's Insights**
Useful resource sold through an inefficient acquisition route.

**5. Gamescom Special Pack 5 – Tokugawa/Hammurabi**
Poor value compared with alternative ways of acquiring the featured Immortal fragments.

## A Note on GTarcade Purchases

There is one extra factor worth checking before purchasing any Gamescom pack: **external-shop recharge rewards**.

GTarcade has historically attached additional rewards and recharge milestones to purchases made through its official shop, and its current voucher listings show cumulative top-up rewards at several monthly spending thresholds. That means the effective value of a Gamescom purchase can be slightly higher if it simultaneously pushes your account across a recharge milestone.

That does not suddenly make Pack 5 efficient, but it is worth checking before deciding where to make a purchase.

## Conclusion

The **Gamescom 2026 Special Packs are a mixed promotion rather than a blanket discount event**.

The clear winner is **Gamescom Special Pack 2**. Thirty-two Theia's Roulette tickets for $9.99 is substantially better than the usual ticket ratio and gives players pursuing Holy or Shadow Dragon development a straightforward reason to buy.

**Pack 1** is also worthwhile when those 60 selectable Artifact Schematic Fragments directly advance an artifact you are already building. I would not buy them merely to sit in inventory, but targeted artifact progression at this price is respectable.

After those two, the value drops quickly.

The **Master's Insights pack** loses badly to long-term Campaign Supplies. **City of Glory** is more defensible as a cosmetic purchase than a progression purchase, particularly because other $50 festival offerings provide broader rewards. Finally, the **$99.99 Tokugawa/Hammurabi pack** is difficult to justify when Elemental Bond offers a much cheaper route toward comparable Immortal progression.

The advertised discounts range from **48% to 95%**, but Infinity Kingdom veterans should know better than to judge a pack by the percentage printed above it. Compare the contents against the cheapest repeatable source of the same progression.

For Gamescom 2026, that comparison produces a fairly simple answer:

**Buy the Roulette Tickets if you need dragon progression. Consider the Artifact Schematics if you have a specific artifact target. Treat everything else as optional or inefficient.**
