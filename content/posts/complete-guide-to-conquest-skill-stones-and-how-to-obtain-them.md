Conquest is where Infinity Kingdom’s skill system really opens up. More skills become available through the Wisdom Well, the Cinder Shop continues to expand, Legion of Frostborne adds another group of Ability Stones and paid recharge chains offer several additional selection chests.

The difficulty is no longer finding skills. It is understanding which currency belongs to which shop, what can be obtained for free and which skills are worth targeting first. Patch 2.9.9 has made this slightly more confusing by moving several skills between seasons, so this guide provides an updated overview of what is currently available during Conquest.

## What Changed in Patch 2.9.9?

Patch 2.9.9 changed the season in which four Wisdom Well skills are released:

- **Arcane Hunter** now becomes available in Conquest instead of Legendary Season.
- **Divine Blessing** now becomes available in Conquest instead of Legendary Season.
- **Eternal Battlelust** now becomes available in Conquest instead of Legendary Season.

On a related note, while not directly relevant to conquest players, its worth noting that **Energy Master**y now becomes available in Season 2 instead of Conquest. Moving Energy Master to Season 2 does not mean it disappears when a server enters Conquest. It simply becomes available earlier and remains part of the available skill progression afterward.

Likewise, it deserves to be quickly noted, that the Patch 2.9.9 skill, **Psychic Vortex** is released through the Wish Shop during **Legendary Season**, meaning it is **not**available to ordinary Conquest servers.

Arcane Hunter and Divine Blessing also expand the number of Conquest options, while Energy Master becoming available in Season 2 allows developing servers to start working on one of the game’s strongest energy-support skills before they reach Conquest.

Each stone costs **17 Cinders**, meaning that unlocking a skill from zero requires:

### 60 stones × 17 Cinders = 1,020 Cinders

Unwanted Ability Stones can be sold for Cinders, but this is not a direct one-for-one exchange into another Ability Stone. Selling one unwanted stone gives one Cinder, while purchasing one target stone costs 17 Cinders. Recycling remains useful, but it should be understood as slow account progression rather than a free skill swap.

This is why Cinders should be concentrated on important skills. Weakening Curse, Wind Rage, Full Coverage, Siren’s Gaze and other established options may be worth building, but the correct priority depends entirely on your marches.

## Legion of Frostborne Ability Stones

The Legion’s Vender allows players to purchase Ability Stones using Cold Silver earned during KvK. Each stone costs **800 Cold Silver**, and a full 60-stone unlock therefore costs: **60 × 800 = 48,000 Cold Silver**.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-213-1024x529.png)

The Conquest KvK selection includes:

- Abundant Spirit
- Assembly Charge
- Berserker
- Full Coverage
- Siren’s Gaze
- Snipe
- Spell Disintegrator
- Stormeye
- Unforeseen Attack
- Victorious Pursuit
- Weakness Protection
- Wind Rage

For most developing accounts, these skills should take priority over ordinary resources, speedups and artifact materials sold in the same shop. **Wind Rage, Assembly Charge and Abundant Spirit** are especially important skills to secure as early as possible.

If you do not need one of the available KvK skills, its stones can still be purchased and sold for Cinders. This gives veteran players another way to make slow progress toward Cinder Shop skills, although the 17-Cinder purchase price means the conversion is expensive and should not be mistaken for an equal exchange.

Ancient Remnant chests should not be included as a Conquest Ability Stone source. In the current version, these chests focus on Holy and Shadow Immortal fragments and artifact-related rewards rather than the old selection of KvK skill stones.

## Recharge Chain Selection Chest VII

Conquest recharge chains can provide **Ability Stone Selection Chest VII**. The usual seven-day structure awards five chests on purchased days, with an additional 25-chest reward after completing five qualifying days.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-212-1024x556.png)

Selection Chest VII contains:

- Blade Dodge
- Blood Frenzy
- Chaos Storm
- Heart of Arcana
- Iron Curtain
- Lightning Strike
- Master’s Aegis
- Psionic Tide
- Soul Synergy
- Time to Shine

This is a paid shortcut rather than a free progression source, but it offers much more control than gambling for a specific Wisdom Well drop. Players who already spend regularly can use these chains to finish a targeted skill, particularly when they are only missing a small number of stones.

As always, inspect the active chest before purchasing. Selection chest contents can change as new skills are introduced, and the in-game reward preview should be treated as the final authority.

## How to Plan Your Skill Progression

Free-to-play and very low-spending players should focus on reliable sources. Complete the most important KvK skills with Cold Silver, collect Cinders gradually and avoid dividing resources between too many unfinished skills. A completed good skill is far more useful than having 20 stones for five different skills.

Moderate spenders can combine targeted Wisdom Well participation with the Cinder Shop and occasional recharge chains. The main advantage of spending in Conquest is access to legendary skills alongside Selection Chest I, so Wisdom Well Coins generally have more long-term potential here than they do during earlier seasons.

High spenders can push the legendary pool more aggressively, but even then it is worth planning around complete builds rather than collecting every new skill automatically. Eternal Battlelust may be exceptional for a Medusa composition, for example, but that does not make it equally valuable for every march.

## Should You Buy Skills Before Conquest?

Buying a skill during Season 1 or Season 2 is often a question of timing rather than permanent exclusivity. Some skills that are expensive or difficult to obtain early become available through the Cinder Shop or other sources later.

If obtaining a skill now allows you to win important fights, improve your arena position or carry your alliance through a competitive season, paying for early access can be worthwhile. If you are not competing heavily, waiting until Conquest will often be considerably more efficient.

Patch 2.9.9 reinforces this point. Energy Master now arrives in Season 2, while Arcane Hunter, Divine Blessing and Eternal Battlelust arrive in Conquest instead of forcing players to wait for Legendary Season. Skill availability is not static, and paying a premium for early access always carries the possibility that the same skill becomes easier to obtain later.

## Summary

Conquest gives players access to a much wider Ability Stone economy through the Wisdom Well, Selection Chest I, Cinder Shop, Legion’s Vender and recharge selection chests. Patch 2.9.9 expands that economy by bringing **Arcane Hunter, Divine Blessing and Eternal Battlelust** forward from Legendary Season, while **Energy Master** now begins appearing as early as Season 2.

The best approach is to choose skills based on the marches you actually use, finish one target at a time and avoid assuming that every early premium skill will remain exclusive forever. Players who plan across several seasons will almost always obtain more useful skills for the same amount of money, Cold Silver and Cinders.
