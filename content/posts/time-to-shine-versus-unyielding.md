## Time to Shine vs Unyielding – Which Critical Skill Should You Choose?

With **Time to Shine** joining the Tower of Knowledge, one question immediately started popping up across the community: **Is it better than Unyielding?**

It's a fair question. Both skills revolve around critical hits, both are aimed at physical damage dealers, and both are capable of producing impressive damage numbers. At first glance they even look like they compete for the exact same skill slot.

After testing both extensively—and after watching how they're performing across high-level PvP—the answer isn't as simple as saying one is strictly better than the other.

The truth is that they reward completely different approaches to building your march.

---

## Understanding the Difference

The biggest mistake players make is looking only at the Critical Rate. Yes, Time to Shine gives **35% Critical Rate**, while Unyielding gives **50%**. If that were the whole story, Unyielding would obviously win. But it isn't.

The real comparison looks like this:

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-93-1024x139.png)

That completely changes how each skill performs. One is designed around **early pressure**. The other is designed around **winning long fights.**

---

## Time to Shine – Reliable Damage From the First Second

Time to Shine is wonderfully simple.

As soon as the battle starts, your carry immediately gains:

- 35% Critical Rate
- 30% Physical Damage

There is no waiting, no trigger, no pre-requisite. Your very first normal attack and your very first Ultimate already benefit from both buffs.

That reliability is incredibly valuable in today's meta, where many battles are decided during the opening Ultimate rotation. If your goal is deleting the enemy carry before they get going, Time to Shine helps you do exactly that.

---

## Unyielding – Delayed Power for Longer Battles

Unyielding works very differently. Nothing happens until your immortal falls below **70% troops**.

Only then do you receive:

- 50% Critical Rate
- Self-healing based on physical damage dealt

When that trigger finally happens, the skill becomes incredibly powerful. The problem is getting there. Modern Infinity Kingdom battles are often extremely polarized. Either your carry barely takes damage, or they lose 80% of their troops in one Ultimate rotation.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-99-1024x577.png)

Neither scenario is ideal for Unyielding. If your immortal never reaches 70%, the skill contributes absolutely nothing. If they drop from 100% to nearly dead in one burst, there often isn't enough time for the healing to matter. That's why Unyielding feels amazing in some reports and completely invisible in others.

That being said, Unyielding can be very powerful! If you are in a situation where you are not bursted down quickly but instead loose HP/Troop count steadily you will be able to get huge value off of this skill. This works especially well on frontline immortals who will usually take damage and have their troop count diminish more "steadily" is where there is a strong use for this skill. E.g. on an Alexander or a Poseidon you are more reliably getting to 70% instead of being bursted down to 40% right off the bat. But it also works well on backliners if against e.g. Alex with Assembly and Knife Through Butter, simply because your backliner will take damage often and slowly diminish troop count, getting the trigger at 70% instead of after being burst well below that.

Another added bonus of Unyielding over Time to Shine is the self-healing. On Immortals that deal many damage instances, this can climb to quite a lot of healing! E.g. Alexander's or Poseidon's.

---

## The Current PvP Meta Favors Time to Shine

One of the biggest reasons Time to Shine has become so attractive is simply the direction Infinity Kingdom has taken over the past year. Between Tower of Knowledge skills, stronger artifacts, insignias and ever-increasing account progression, the overall pace of PvP has accelerated considerably. Many fights are effectively decided during the first rotation of ultimates, where one side gains an early advantage that snowballs into the rest of the battle.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-95-1024x579.png)

That naturally plays into Time to Shine's strengths. Because its bonuses are active from the moment combat begins, your damage dealer benefits from both the additional Critical Rate and Physical Damage immediately. There's no waiting for a trigger condition and no uncertainty over whether the skill will activate at all. Every attack and every early Ultimate has a higher chance to crit, making the skill particularly valuable for players who rely on early pressure to secure victories.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-96-1024x352.png)

---

## Unyielding Is Still an Excellent Skill

That doesn't mean Unyielding has suddenly become a bad investment. In fact, once it activates, it offers one of the strongest offensive buffs available to physical damage dealers.

A 50% increase to Critical Rate is enormous. It wasn't that long ago that players happily dedicated an entire skill slot to Rage simply because it provided a 15% Critical Rate bonus. Unyielding offers more than three times that amount, while also adding a healing component that can dramatically improve survivability in prolonged engagements.

The catch, of course, is that you have to earn those bonuses. If your immortal never falls below the 70% troop threshold, the skill contributes nothing. Likewise, if they are burst from full health to near death before the effect has a chance to matter, the value is greatly diminished. Unyielding is incredibly powerful when the conditions are right, but those conditions don't exist in every fight.

---

## Where Unyielding Really Excels

Although many players immediately think of Khan or Hippolyta when discussing Unyielding, I've actually found some of the best results on frontline physical attackers. Alexander is probably the best example.

Unlike a traditional backline carry, Alexander is expected to spend the entire battle taking damage. That means he reliably drops below the activation threshold without instantly dying, allowing him to take full advantage of both the Critical Rate increase and the healing effect. Combined with his naturally high attack speed, the sustain becomes surprisingly noticeable, as he continues dealing consistent damage while repeatedly restoring his own health.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-100-1024x584.png)

Instead of functioning as a burst carry, Unyielding turns Alexander into a frontline duelist that becomes increasingly difficult to remove as the fight goes on. It's a completely different playstyle from Time to Shine, but one that can be extremely effective when built correctly.

---

## Illusion Battlegrounds Changes the Equation

Illusion Battlegrounds is probably the game mode where Unyielding has impressed me the most. Although the matches are relatively short, they're generally far less burst-heavy than high-end Kingdom PvP, where a single critical Ultimate can remove an immortal almost instantly. Frontline immortals spend much more of the battle exchanging blows, which gives Unyielding the time it needs to activate and generate value.

This is especially noticeable on Alexander. Once the healing starts triggering consistently, he becomes surprisingly difficult to bring down, often surviving long enough to win one-on-one engagements that would otherwise be lost. The raw healing numbers may not seem spectacular at first glance, but within the context of Illusion Battlegrounds they represent a significant amount of effective health and frequently make the difference between winning and losing a duel.

---

## Healing Can Be Countered

One limitation that shouldn't be overlooked is that Unyielding's healing is not guaranteed to provide full value. Teams that apply healing reduction, such as Serious Wounds effects, or immortals like King Emperor that interfere with healing mechanics, can significantly reduce the sustain that makes the skill so attractive in the first place.

Even in those situations, Unyielding still grants its substantial Critical Rate bonus, so the skill certainly isn't useless. However, a large part of its overall value comes from combining additional damage with increased survivability. If the healing component is removed from the equation, the gap between Unyielding and Time to Shine becomes noticeably smaller. Time to Shine, by comparison, has no such weakness. Its bonuses are permanent, unconditional and available from the first second of every battle.

---

## Should You Ever Run Both Together?

I've experimented with running both skills at the same time, and while the idea sounds appealing on paper, I don't think it's the optimal approach.

The two skills are trying to accomplish different things. Time to Shine is built around maximising your damage output from the opening seconds of combat, whereas Unyielding is designed to reward immortals that remain alive long enough to become stronger later in the fight. In practice, that means they often compete for the same role instead of complementing each other.

I generally find better results by pairing each skill with abilities that reinforce its intended playstyle. Time to Shine works naturally alongside offensive skills such as Corrosive Power, Energy Mastery or Fighting Master, where the goal is to maximise early burst. Unyielding, on the other hand, performs best in defensive or sustain-oriented builds that are specifically designed to survive long enough for the activation condition to become relevant.

---

## Which One Would I Prioritise?

If I had to recommend one skill for the average player, I would still lean towards Time to Shine. It delivers immediate value in every battle, fits naturally into the current burst-oriented meta and doesn't rely on specific conditions before it starts contributing. Whether you're using Khan, Zeon, Hippolyta or another physical carry, you'll almost always benefit from having more Critical Rate and Physical Damage from the very first attack.

That said, I don't think Unyielding should be viewed as a weaker version of Time to Shine. It's simply a more specialised skill. Players who favour durable frontline attackers, sustain-focused compositions or game modes such as Illusion Battlegrounds may find that Unyielding actually produces better results because those environments allow the skill to function exactly as intended. When your build is designed around surviving below the 70% troop threshold instead of avoiding it, Unyielding becomes a genuinely powerful alternative rather than a consolation prize.

---

## Final Thoughts

After spending time testing both skills, I don't think this is really a question of which one is objectively stronger. It's much more a question of what you're asking your march to do.

If your strategy revolves around putting your opponent under pressure from the very first Ultimate, **Time to Shine** is simply the better fit. The skill provides immediate value in every single battle, regardless of the matchup, and its combination of Critical Rate and Physical Damage makes it one of the most reliable offensive skills available for physical carries. In the current PvP environment, where many fights are decided within the opening 20–30 seconds, that consistency is incredibly valuable.

**Unyielding**, on the other hand, rewards a completely different style of play. Rather than helping you win the opening exchange, it turns an immortal into a much stronger fighter once they've weathered the initial burst. When used on the right immortal—particularly durable frontline attackers like Alexander—or in game modes such as Illusion Battlegrounds where fights naturally last longer, it can produce some outstanding results. The combination of a massive 50% Critical Rate increase and self-sustain makes for a very difficult immortal to finish off.

For most players, I would still recommend **Time to Shine as the first priority**. It fits into more marches, performs well in virtually every PvP mode, and never leaves you wondering whether the skill will activate. If you're investing valuable Tower of Knowledge resources into a single offensive skill, reliability goes a long way.

That certainly doesn't mean Unyielding should be overlooked. In fact, I think it's one of those skills that becomes stronger the more deliberately you build around it. Players who enjoy experimenting with bruiser-style frontline builds or sustain-focused compositions will likely get far more value out of it than the average player, and I wouldn't be surprised if we continue to see new uses for it as the meta evolves.

At the end of the day, these skills aren't competing for the exact same role. **Time to Shine is about winning the fight before your opponent can recover. Unyielding is about surviving long enough to become the stronger march.** Both approaches are perfectly viable—it simply comes down to which one better matches the way you want to play.
