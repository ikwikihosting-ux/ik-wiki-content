## Introduction

Deep Sea Venture has arrived in Infinity Kingdom, introducing a familiar pinball-style reward system centered around Pearls and Scales. At first glance, the event appears straightforward. Players spend Pearls, launch them onto the board, collect rewards, and exchange Scales for Immortals, Artifacts, and other items in the event shop.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image-20-1024x563.png)

The mechanic itself is simple. The challenge comes from evaluating the event's actual value. During the first few days of any new event, reward efficiency is often difficult to calculate because players do not yet have enough data to establish reliable averages.

After examining the event structure, shop rewards, bundle options, and early player reports, several strengths and concerns already stand out.

---

## How Deep Sea Venture Works

The gameplay loop follows the same basic concept used in previous pinball-style events.

1. Obtain Pearls.
2. Launch Pearls onto the board.
3. Receive rewards based on where they land.
4. Collect Scales and other materials.
5. Exchange Scales in the event shop.

For experienced players, there is very little learning curve. If you have played Crimson Moonlight or Stardust-style events before, the system will feel immediately familiar.

The real question is not how the event works, but how much value players receive from each Pearl spent.

---

## The Biggest Issue: Hidden Reward Distribution

One of the first things players noticed is the way reward odds are displayed.

The event shows overall probabilities for reward categories. For example, Scales may be listed with a combined drop rate.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image-22.png)

It is not quite clear on the coloring either, but if you look closely you can see the slight background colors around each rewards icon frames.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image-21-1024x151.png)

However, the game does not currently reveal the individual probability of receiving:

- 5 Scales
- 10 Scales
- 25 Scales
- 50 Scales
- 100 Scales

Instead, players only see the combined probability of receiving any Scale reward.

This creates a problem for anyone trying to calculate expected value.

For example, two events could both display a 37% Scale drop rate while producing completely different outcomes depending on how frequently higher-value rewards appear.

Without access to the detailed distribution, accurate cost analysis remains difficult.

---

## Early Shop

The event shop focuses heavily on Holy and Shadow Immortal progression.

Available rewards include:

- Holy Immortal shards
- Shadow Immortal shards
- Artifact fragments
- Various supporting resources

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image-23-1024x580.png)

One important detail is that many purchases are limited.

For example:

- Immortal shards are purchased in batches.
- Daily purchase caps appear to restrict progression speed.
- Artifact fragment purchases are similarly limited.

This means that even players willing to spend heavily cannot instantly complete certain rewards through a single day of purchases.

Whether these limits reset daily or follow another schedule remains something players should monitor during the event's first cycle.

---

## Free-to-Play Perspective

At launch, one of the most notable differences between Deep Sea Venture and Rune Factory is the absence of a gem bundle.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image-24-1024x517.png)

This has significant implications.

Most successful Infinity Kingdom events provide at least some way for free-to-play players to convert saved Gems into event progress. Rune Factory is a good example of this approach.

Deep Sea Venture currently offers no equivalent option.

As a result:

- Free-to-play players have very limited participation.
- Event progression is heavily tied to spending.
- Long-term accessibility is significantly lower than Rune Factory.

Unless changes are introduced later, this may become one of the event's largest criticisms.

---

## Comparing Deep Sea Venture to Rune Factory

Naturally, most players will compare Deep Sea Venture to Rune Factory because both events target valuable progression items.

Based on early player reports, Deep Sea Venture currently appears less efficient. That however, is a quite early estimate as this event does not show clear drop rate percentages nor has it been thoroughly tested by players yet. Stay tuned for more details as we test!

---

## Should Light Spenders Participate?

For light spenders, patience may be the best strategy.

There are two reasons for this. First, the event's reward distribution is not yet fully understood. Second, early efficiency estimates suggest that equivalent progression costs noticeably more than established alternatives.

In practice, most light spenders benefit from investing in events where expected returns are already known and proven. Rune Factory currently holds that advantage.

Unless additional information improves Deep Sea Venture's projected value, many players may find better returns elsewhere.

---

## Player Perspective

After years of watching new Infinity Kingdom events launch, one pattern appears repeatedly: initial versions often receive adjustments after player feedback.

Rune Factory itself is a good example. Many players were skeptical when it first arrived, yet it eventually became one of the most reliable events for Chaos, Holy, Shadow, and Artifact progression.

Deep Sea Venture may follow a similar path.

At the moment, however, the event feels unfinished from an evaluation standpoint. The lack of detailed reward probabilities makes meaningful cost analysis difficult, and the absence of gem participation leaves free-to-play players with very little reason to engage.

Before committing significant resources, I would personally wait for more community data and observe whether any adjustments are introduced during the first event cycle.

---

## Conclusion Summary

Deep Sea Venture introduces a familiar and easy-to-understand pinball mechanic, but its overall value remains unclear.

### Early Takeaways

- The gameplay itself is simple and accessible.
- Reward odds lack important detail.
- Holy and Shadow progression appears expensive compared to Rune Factory.
- Artifact acquisition appears significantly more expensive.
- No gem bundle currently limits free-to-play participation.
- Light spenders may want to wait for additional data before investing heavily.

For now, Deep Sea Venture should be viewed as an event worth watching rather than an event worth rushing into. The coming days will reveal whether the reward structure can compete with established favorites such as Rune Factory.
