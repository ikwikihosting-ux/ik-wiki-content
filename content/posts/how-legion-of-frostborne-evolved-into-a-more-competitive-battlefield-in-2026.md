## Introduction

Legion of Frostborne has changed considerably since its early Conquest seasons. Some improvements, such as the Federation system and revised matchmaking criteria, were introduced through official updates. Others have happened more quietly, without much explanation from the developers.

One of the biggest changes has been the gradual shift toward fewer, but much more populated, KvK worlds.

While this adjustment was never prominently documented, veteran players quickly noticed the difference. Modern Legion of Frostborne seasons consistently place more active alliances into the same battlefield, creating wars that feel larger, more competitive, and far less predictable than they did a few years ago.

Looking back, it's one of the best changes the mode has received.

---

## The Early Days of Legion of Frostborne

Older Legion of Frostborne seasons were spread across a much larger number of separate KvK worlds.

Instead of concentrating servers into a handful of competitive battles, the matchmaking system created many individual worlds running simultaneously. Although this allowed more KvKs to take place at once, it also spread active alliances much more thinly.

Most battlefields followed a familiar pattern.

One alliance—or occasionally two—stood well above everyone else in terms of activity, organisation, and spending. Once those alliances established control of the map, the outcome often became predictable.

Many cities were captured with little resistance, major objectives were secured early, and large parts of the map became relatively quiet as the season progressed.

There were certainly memorable wars during this period, but they were the exception rather than the rule.

---

## A Quiet Change to Matchmaking

At some point, the matchmaking landscape began to change.

Without any major announcement, the number of active Legion of Frostborne worlds appeared to decrease. Instead of creating many smaller KvKs, the game began grouping more active servers into fewer battlefields.

Whether this was a direct adjustment to matchmaking or part of broader improvements behind the scenes, the result was immediately noticeable.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-83-1024x483.png)

Every KvK suddenly felt busier.

Instead of encountering one dominant alliance supported by several weaker ones, it became increasingly common to see multiple established alliances competing within the same world.

For players who enjoy large-scale warfare, this completely changed the character of Legion of Frostborne.

---

## More Powerhouses, Fewer Easy Victories

Perhaps the biggest difference is the quality of competition.

In the past, a dominant alliance could often dictate the pace of an entire season. Once they secured the central objectives, there were relatively few opponents capable of mounting a serious challenge.

Today's KvKs rarely feel that straightforward.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-85-1024x540.png)

A single battlefield can now contain several highly organised alliances spread across multiple factions. Each of those alliances brings experienced leadership, active members, and enough strength to influence the outcome of the war.

Winning no longer comes from simply having the strongest alliance.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-86-1024x567.png)

Success depends on outperforming several strong opponents over the course of an entire season.

---

## Every Stage of the War Feels Active

One of the biggest improvements is the overall pace of Legion of Frostborne.

Because more active alliances share the same battlefield, there is much less downtime. Border conflicts continue throughout the season. Cities remain contested. Major objectives attract genuine competition instead of becoming routine captures.

Even after the initial expansion phase, there are usually multiple fronts where meaningful battles are taking place. From my experience, modern KvKs simply feel alive for much longer than they did under the older format.

---

## Diplomacy Has Become More Important

The increased level of competition has naturally placed greater emphasis on diplomacy.

When every faction contains several capable alliances, coordination becomes just as valuable as military strength. Strong alliances still need to work together, agree on city ownership, and coordinate attacks against rival factions. Internal disagreements are much more costly when every opponent is capable of exploiting them.

The most successful servers are rarely the ones with the highest power alone. More often, they're the ones that organise effectively before the map even opens.

---

## A Healthier Competitive Environment

The modern format has also given more alliances meaningful roles throughout the season.

Older KvKs often revolved around a single dominant alliance while everyone else played a supporting role. Today, multiple alliances contribute to capturing cities, defending territory, reinforcing allies, and contesting key objectives. Success is spread across a larger part of the server rather than resting on one group of players.

That makes Legion of Frostborne feel less like a showcase for a few powerhouse alliances and more like a genuine server-wide war.

---

## Looking Back

No matchmaking system will ever produce perfectly balanced KvKs, and some worlds will always be stronger than others.

Even so, the gradual shift toward fewer, larger Legion of Frostborne battlefields has been one of the most positive developments the game has seen.

More active alliances now compete within each world, creating longer campaigns, more meaningful diplomacy, and far more opportunities for large-scale PvP. Instead of spending weeks waiting for the next major battle, players are far more likely to find action throughout the season.

The developers may never have officially highlighted this change, but its impact is difficult to miss. Legion of Frostborne has become a more engaging, more competitive, and ultimately more rewarding experience because of it.
