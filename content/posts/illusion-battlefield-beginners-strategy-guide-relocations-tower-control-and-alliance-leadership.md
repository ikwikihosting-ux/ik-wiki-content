## Plan Your Opening Around Positioning

Before the battle begins, decide which objectives your alliance intends to contest and assign responsibility for each area. Every player should know their opening destination, their relocation marker and the commander they follow.

Then consider starting positions. Players with favourable spawns can secure important spaces before the enemy arrives. Use those advantages when assigning the frontline around a tower, rather than distributing players purely by power or sending everyone wherever numbers appear lowest.

Your strongest attacker may be valuable in several areas. A player who can win a critical relocation race has an immediate job that someone starting farther away may be unable to perform.

### Place Precise Relocation Markers

Markers should identify an exact position, allowing players to follow the plan with minimal adjustment. Align them with the bottom-left corner of the intended relocation footprint and check that friendly relocations do not overlap.

Prioritize positions close enough to support the objective and one another. A castle established next to a tower can help reinforce it, challenge nearby enemies and deny space to incoming relocations. An isolated castle farther away may struggle to contribute before its teammates come under pressure.

Prepare a fallback assignment as well. Players whose opening relocation fails should report the situation and receive a useful next move quickly.

## Treat Relocation Denial as a Core Job

Blocking or destroying an enemy relocation prevents that player from establishing their intended position. It can remove an attacker, delay reinforcements or stop the opponent from surrounding your tower.

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261020-152154-6fppzi.png)

Assign players to watch incoming relocations while others organize rallies and prepare the objective fight. If everyone focuses on attacking the nearest enemy castle, important arrivals can go unchallenged.

The immediate goal is to create a local numbers advantage. Six players who successfully establish themselves can control an area against a larger group whose arrivals are repeatedly blocked or destroyed.

Once an enemy foothold is removed, occupy the useful positions it leaves behind. Otherwise, the opponent may return to the same area and rebuild its presence.

## Use the Central Tower to Support Your Wider Strategy

The Central Tower combines high scoring value with a location that supports movement toward other objectives. Controlling it provides points and a useful base for reinforcing battles elsewhere.

This matters when players need to replace teammates who have been forced out. A group established near the center can often respond more efficiently than players committed to a distant outer tower.

Your center allocation should fit the wider plan. Decide how many players are needed to establish control, who will remain to defend and who can rotate once another objective needs help.

If you concede the center, you need a realistic scoring alternative. That usually means securing enough other objectives quickly and preventing the opponent from using its central position to challenge them. A plan that depends on several captures must account for the time needed to achieve all of them.

### Release Players From Secure Objectives

An uncontested tower rarely needs the same commitment as a tower under sustained pressure. Once an objective is secure, reassess who needs to remain and who can contribute elsewhere.

Keep suitable coverage and make sure you surround your objectives before giving specific players new responsonsibilities. 

![Screenshot](/uploads/screenshot-20261020-152300-fy32mi.png)

Avoid leaving a large group waiting at a safe objective while another group loses a contested position.

The same principle applies at the center. Its usefulness as a staging area depends on players being ready to move when needed.

## Defend the Tower and the Castles Around It

A tower defense depends on the players supporting the garrison. If the enemy removes your nearby castles, reinforcement becomes harder and the opponent gains better access to the objective.

Maintain positions that allow teammates to reinforce one another, challenge incoming relocations and organize rallies. Protect the players doing that supporting work, including those carrying useful capture skills.

### Replace Weakened Frontline Players

Watch Prosperity before a frontline castle is close to being forced out. A weakened player who moves behind teammates can continue helping with rallies and garrisons, while a healthier player takes the exposed position.

Coordinate the swap. The replacement needs a clear destination, and both players must avoid obstructing friendly relocations. Waiting until the frontline player is removed gives the enemy a better chance to occupy the space first.

Keeping players established around the objective preserves your options. A defender who remains nearby can contribute to the next exchange instead of spending time returning from elsewhere.

### Create an Opening Before Repeating a Capture Attempt

If both alliances can continually interrupt occupation, sending more marches into the same situation may produce little progress. Identify the enemy positions that allow those interruptions and coordinate pressure against them.

Controlling an additional side of the tower improves access and can make it harder for defenders to respond quickly enough. Coordinate the push so newly arriving players have support rather than leaving isolated castles around the objective.

Once a workable opening exists, call the capture attempt clearly. Troop arrivals and support skills such as **Quick Occupy** should be coordinated around the same attempt.

## Attack the Enemy’s Supporting Positions

A defended tower is harder to take when the opposing backline can organize rallies and reinforce teammates without coming under pressure.

Identify which castles sustain that defense. Depending on the situation, removing a support player or cutting off an arrival route can create more progress than repeatedly attacking the tower from one direction.

Approaching from additional sides can force defenders to divide their attention, but the arriving players need enough support to survive. Surrounding the objective with isolated, vulnerable castles gives the enemy opportunities to remove them individually.

Each group should understand its immediate job: establish a position, block arrivals, remove a particular castle or support the capture. That gives commanders a way to judge whether the attack is progressing.

## Know When to Reinforce and When to Withdraw

A failed opening does not justify abandoning an objective automatically. It does require a new assessment before committing more players.

Identify what reinforcements would change. Can they secure a position, remove an exposed enemy or help complete the occupation? If they are likely to repeat the same failed approach, use them where they can contribute sooner.

Withdrawing can preserve players for a more useful fight or allow a coordinated return later. Communicate the new assignment so the group moves together instead of returning individually to the lost position.

### Defending to Buy Time

An objective can remain worth defending even when eventually losing it is likely. If holding it delays a larger enemy force while your alliance scores elsewhere, the defense may still contribute to victory.

Make that purpose clear. Players assigned to delay should understand how long they need to hold and when they should leave. Their success is measured by the time and points gained for the alliance.

## Organize Rotations and Home Defense

A rotation is the reassignment of players from one area to another. The receiving commander should know where they can arrive and what they are expected to do.

Specify the number needed, the destination and the immediate task. Leave named players responsible for the objective being vacated.

Short calls are easier to follow during a fight:

* “Tower 1 is secure. Four players follow the center markers.”
* “Stop relocating to Tower 4. Reinforce center.”
* “Block the incoming relocation south of the tower.”
* “Home tower needs two defenders. Others hold position.”

### Keep Home Defense Responsive

Assign enough coverage to spot an invasion and contest its first arrivals. The number required depends on the threat, your defenders and how quickly help can reach them.

A small guard preserves players for contested objectives only when it is actively monitoring the area and has a realistic reinforcement plan. Report incoming relocations before the enemy establishes several castles nearby.

You can also keep a few players available for changing assignments. Give them useful work near positions from which they can respond, and avoid committing every available player to a distant fight at the same time.

## Let the Scoreboard Guide Your Decisions

As the match develops, review the score difference, current scoring rates and remaining time. Capturing a tower helps only if its rewards can still affect the result.

If your current holdings are sufficient to win, consider the risk before moving defenders away to pursue another objective. If you are behind, identify which change in control would close the gap and concentrate on making that change possible.

A late capture can come after the opportunity to recover has already passed. Equally, a tower held for most of the match may have done its job even if the enemy eventually takes it.

Reassess after every major capture, lost objective or rotation. Tell each group whether its current task is to capture, defend or delay, and keep that instruction tied to the score.

## A Practical Plan for Alliance Leadership

**Before the battle**, confirm attendance, assign commanders and prepare opening markers. Coordinate strategy skills and give each group a fallback assignment.

**During the opening**, check who actually establishes a position. Adjust allocations around successful arrivals rather than assuming every assigned player reached their destination.

**As objective fights develop**, protect supporting castles, replace weakened frontline players and coordinate capture attempts. Release players from secure objectives when another area offers a more useful job.

**In the final phase**, make the scoring plan explicit. Every group should understand what it needs to hold, capture or delay for the alliance to win.

## Final Thoughts

Good Illusion Battlefield leadership keeps players useful throughout the match. That means securing positions, preventing enemy arrivals and moving reinforcements while they can still change the outcome.

The most practical improvements are habits your alliance can repeat: precise markers, clear local command, earlier replacement calls and a willingness to change assignments when a position collapses. Build those into your preparation, then review which decisions cost you time after each match.
