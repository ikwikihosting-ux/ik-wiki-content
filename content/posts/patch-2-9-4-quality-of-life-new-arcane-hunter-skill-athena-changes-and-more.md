## Introduction

Patch 2.9.4 is, on the surface, a quality-of-life update. Most of the changes improve clarity and usability rather than raw power. However, underneath that, there are two adjustments that point clearly toward ongoing developer intent: a continued push away from magic-dominated compositions and toward physical damage setups.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-25-1024x572.png)

This direction isn’t new. It started around the release of Apollo, accelerated significantly with Medusa, and has continued through skill design. With Arcane Hunter and the Athena adjustment, we’re seeing that trend reinforced again—this time through both offensive scaling and defensive counterplay.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-17-1024x368.png)

## Resource and Speed-Up Tracking

This change is purely quality of life, but it’s one that has been overdue for a long time.

For years, players have had to manually track their speed-ups and resources, especially when planning KvK participation or long upgrade cycles. The lack of visibility often led to inefficient usage—burning universals too early, misjudging healing capacity, or simply not knowing what was available without digging through the inventory.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-18-1024x466.png)

To give some context, tools were created outside the game to solve this. I built a resource and speed-up calculator for the wiki some time ago because there was consistent demand from players trying to plan more accurately.

With this update, that process is now built into the game. You can quickly see your totals and make decisions accordingly.

In practice, this won’t make you stronger, but it will make your progression smoother. It’s especially valuable during KvK, where proper allocation of speed-ups directly determines how long you can stay active in fights.

## Zuma Tower Transparency Update

The Zuma Tower change is minor. The event was already functional and reasonably transparent, but this adds another layer of clarity by showing reward claims directly.

While I haven’t seen a full Zuma cycle post-patch yet, the intention is clear: reduce guesswork and make progress easier to track.

In reality, this doesn’t change how you play Zuma. It just makes it easier to judge whether continuing to invest is worth it. These kinds of changes tend to reduce wasted resources over time, even if the immediate impact feels small.

More transparency is always a positive, especially in events where efficiency matters.

## Arcane Hunter and the Ongoing Meta Shift

Arcane Hunter is the most important addition in this patch, not just because of what it does, but because of what it represents.

At a base level, the skill provides three things: reduced magical damage taken, periodic AoE physical damage every six seconds, and a conditional 30% critical rate bonus when Light Blessing is active.

The numbers themselves are solid. At max level, you’re looking at roughly 300% damage every six seconds, which is consistent pressure rather than burst. The magic damage reduction, around 20% at scale, is also highly relevant in a meta that still leans heavily on magic-based damage.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-19.png)

But the real significance is how this fits into the broader direction of the game.

This shift away from magic started when Apollo was introduced, though at the time he struggled to find a place. It accelerated with Medusa, who effectively defined the modern physical damage meta—especially when paired with skills like Eternal Battlelust. Since then, we’ve seen multiple skill releases that favor physical scaling and reward sustained damage output.

Arcane Hunter continues that trend.

At the same time, it feels like a corrective measure. Apollo has largely fallen out of favor in high-end play, especially compared to Medusa-based setups. This skill gives him a clear avenue back into relevance. While any physical damage dealer can use Arcane Hunter effectively, Apollo benefits the most due to his access to Light Blessing.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-20.png)

That additional 30% crit rate is significant. It turns the skill from steady pressure into something that can consistently spike damage during longer fights. In practice, this could bring Apollo back into contention for optimized builds, particularly in setups designed around sustained engagements.

That said, in top-end whale environments, Medusa is still likely to remain the preferred option. Her scaling and synergy with existing skills are simply more established. But Arcane Hunter does close the gap slightly and broadens viable options.

## Athena Adjustment and Wound Counterplay

Athena’s change is subtle in design but important in context.

Her passive no longer provides 200 initial energy and instead grants immunity to Seriously Wound effects to all allies. This is a direct response to how strong wound mechanics have become, particularly outside of full whale compositions.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-21-1024x636.png)

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-22.png)

Wounds remain one of the most effective ways to shut down sustain. We continue to see immortals like Qin performing well across standard elemental setups largely because of how impactful healing denial is. Even when raw damage isn’t overwhelming, preventing recovery creates consistent advantages over time.

The developers have already tried to address this. In the previous patch, Nine Tails was adjusted to remove wound effects more effectively. However, that change didn’t meaningfully shift her into a dominant healer role.

Athena appears to be the next attempt at solving the same problem.

From a gameplay perspective, this change shifts her role rather than strictly buffing her. Losing the 200 starting energy hurts her early tempo, which was already one of her weaker points. However, the wound immunity introduces a very specific and valuable niche.

In high-end whale fights, this likely won’t change much. Those setups typically don’t rely on wound mechanics in the first place. But in mid to high spender environments—where players mix elemental cores with one or two Chaos immortals—this becomes much more relevant.

We’ve already seen the return of Genghis Khan in many of these setups. Against those builds, Athena can now enable sustain-based compositions that were previously difficult to maintain. By removing wound pressure entirely, she allows healing and mitigation strategies to function as intended.

For players struggling against Khan-based marches, this could open up new defensive options that weren’t viable before.

## Conclusion Summary

Patch 2.9.4 is primarily a quality-of-life update, but it carries clear signals about where the game is heading.

Resource tracking and Zuma improvements reduce friction and improve efficiency, particularly in long events like KvK. Arcane Hunter reinforces the ongoing shift toward physical damage and sustained combat, while also giving Apollo a path back into relevance. Athena’s adjustment addresses the continued strength of wound mechanics, offering a targeted counter that will matter most in mid- to high-tier play.

Individually, these changes are modest. Together, they continue to shape a meta that favors durability, consistency, and careful matchup planning over raw burst or single-strategy dominance.

If you approach the patch with that in mind, the value becomes much clearer.
