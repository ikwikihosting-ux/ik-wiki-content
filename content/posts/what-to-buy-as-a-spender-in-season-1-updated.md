## What to Buy as a Spender in Season 1?

Season 1 is where spending efficiently matters the most, because almost everything looks valuable when your account is new. Immortal fragments, skills, AP, resources and event currencies can all provide immediate power, but not all of them age equally well once you move into later seasons.

The best way to approach Season 1 spending is therefore not simply to ask what makes you stronger today. You also need to consider whether the thing you are buying will still matter once you reach Conquest.

This guide is aimed at **light and mid spenders who want good long-term value**, rather than players trying to buy every possible advantage in Season 1. Whales competing for the top of the server will naturally have different priorities.

It is also worth noting that the Season 1 shop and event structure has changed considerably. **The old Stardust Wheel recommendation is no longer relevant**, as Stardust has been replaced by Zuma and seasonal events. Some other purchases, particularly Cinders and Divine Well spending, also need to be looked at differently under the current system.

---

## Golden Path — Still One of the Best Season 1 Purchases

The **Golden Path** is still one of the easiest purchases to recommend in Season 1, although the reason it is valuable has changed quite a bit over time. It used to be an exceptional source of Purple Soul Crystals for developing Tower of Knowledge skills, but that is no longer the main reason I would buy it. Seasonal events now provide so many Soul Crystals and Philosopher's Stones that both skill development and normal summoning are considerably easier to sustain than they used to be.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-201-1024x575.png)

The real value of Golden Path today is the combination of **immortal progression, artifacts, AP, SP and additional opportunities to obtain immortals that are otherwise awkward to develop early**. The exact rewards depend on the active pass, but this is one of the better places to pick up useful seasonal immortals such as **YSS**, while also progressing artifacts for both elemental and seasonal immortals. Those are rewards that can directly affect the marches you are actually developing rather than simply adding more generic materials to an already plentiful resource pool.

The **AP and SP** are also more important than they may initially appear. Season 1 is largely a race to develop the account quickly enough to keep farming, upgrading and progressing without unnecessary downtime. Having additional AP supports continued PvE farming, while SP helps maintain the pace of development through the season. For an active spender, those resources translate into faster overall account growth rather than a single temporary power increase.

Another reward I value is the **random Immortal Summon Fragments**. Philosopher's Stones are much easier to accumulate now, but not every useful immortal is sitting conveniently in the normal summon pool. **Genghis Khan**, for example, is still harder to obtain because he is not available through regular summoning. Random summon fragments therefore retain some interesting upside: you are not guaranteed the immortal you want, but they give you additional chances at immortals that normal Philosopher's Stone accumulation cannot solve as easily.

This is also why I would no longer sell Golden Path to new players primarily on Soul Crystals. They are certainly useful, and you will still consume plenty of them while upgrading Tower of Knowledge skills, but scarcity is what determines spending value. With modern seasonal events handing out large quantities of Purple Soul Crystals and Philosopher's Stones, neither resource is the bottleneck it once was.

Golden Path remains a very good Season 1 purchase; **the reason has simply changed**. Buy it for the immortals, artifacts, AP/SP and broader progression it provides, with the random Immortal Summon Fragments offering some additional upside for harder-to-acquire immortals. Treat the Soul Crystals as a useful extra rather than the main reason to spend.

## Daily Bundles — Useful, but Don't Overcommit Early

Daily Bundles need a little more nuance than the old recommendation gave them.

Their main attraction is the steady supply of resources, AP and Pearls, but the Pearl side of the equation becomes much more relevant later when artifact empowerment enters the picture. The problem in Season 1 is that a new player usually does not know which immortals they will eventually want to build around.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-202-1024x538.png)

That makes heavy early investment into artifact-related progression somewhat speculative.

For a light spender, **the $1 Daily Bundle is a reasonable stopping point**, and you can continue doing that until Conquest becomes realistically achievable. It gives you some steady account progression without committing much money to a system whose eventual targets may still change.

For larger spenders, the extra AP can certainly be useful if you are farming heavily, but I would no longer describe Daily Bundles as something every mid spender automatically needs to buy every day. Their value depends heavily on how aggressively you are farming and how much you are prepared to spend over an entire season.

In other words, the cheap daily purchase is sensible. Going much further should have a purpose.

## Divine Well / Wisdom Well — Strong Rewards, but Now an Expensive Weekly Commitment

The old version of this guide treated Wisdom Well as something you could gradually approach through cheap bundles and save the currency for later. The current system needs to be evaluated differently because **Divine Well is now weekly rather than monthly**, making consistent participation considerably more expensive.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-203-1024x561.png)

There are still very strong skills available through the system, and spending here can absolutely make an account stronger. The issue is efficiency.

Buying into a premium skill system every week adds up very quickly, particularly during Season 1 when you may not even know what your long-term Conquest march will be. A skill that looks essential for your current Water, Earth or Lightning march may end up sitting unused once your account changes direction.

For a player trying to dominate Season 1, there will naturally be cases where buying the correct skill is worth the premium. If one specific skill completes your main march and you are actively fighting for top rankings, short-term power has real value.

For an efficiency-focused spender, however, I would be much more conservative.

The question should not be, **"Is this a good skill?"** It should be, **"Am I confident I will still use this skill later?"**. If the answer is uncertain, there are usually better places to spend your money. Now to determine which skills are worth getting already in season 1, head on over to the IK-wiki discord where veteran players are more than happy to help you figure out the right skill investments for your build!

## Cinders — The Source Has Changed

The previous recommendation to simply save Cinders until Conquest also needs updating because the way players obtain them has changed.

With the old Stardust system gone, the Cinders you are seeing in Season 1 are most likely coming through the **1050 Grail Box** rather than the old event structure.

That changes how I would think about them. The Grail Box contains several things you may want depending on the stage of your account, so you should not view the purchase purely as "buying Cinders." You are buying the overall box and then deciding how its value fits into your current development.

For an early-season spender, buying **one or two of these boxes** can make sense if the other available rewards are useful to your account. Beyond that point, the efficiency becomes much harder to justify unless you are specifically spending for short-term Season 1 power.

Cinders themselves still have value, but I would not make Cinder acquisition one of the central goals of an efficient Season 1 spending plan.

## GTArcade Shop — Usually Better Than Buying Blindly In-Game

If you are going to spend consistently, it is worth understanding how the **GTArcade shop** interacts with normal purchases. You are generally buying the same underlying game purchases, but spending through GTArcade can contribute toward **cumulative shop rewards**, and the shop also offers **vouchers and discounts**.

That means two players can effectively spend the same amount on the same type of progression while one receives additional value simply because of where the purchase was made. For someone who already knows they are going to spend, this is one of the easier efficiency improvements to make. You are not necessarily increasing your budget; you are trying to extract more from money you were already going to spend.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-204-1024x795.png)

The cumulative rewards are particularly relevant when you are making several purchases across a season. A single small purchase may not make the difference obvious, but repeated spending is where those additional rewards start to matter.

Before making larger purchases directly in-game, I would therefore check whether the equivalent purchase can be made through GTArcade and whether you currently have a useful voucher available.

## Seasonal Events and Zuma

The old guide placed the Stardust Lucky Spin at the very top of the Season 1 priority list. That recommendation should now be removed as **Stardust wheel is no longer the Season 1 opportunity it once was. The old system has been replaced by Zuma and seasonal events**, so there is no longer a reason to tell new spenders to build their entire Season 1 budget around the Stardust Wheel.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-205.png)

Seasonal events should instead be judged individually by their current rewards. Do not assume an event is efficient simply because it is limited or because the game presents it as a special opportunity. Look at what you are actually receiving and ask whether those materials contribute to progression you will still care about several seasons later.

For Season 1 in particular, broadly useful account resources are generally safer than paying heavily for temporary access to something you will eventually obtain much more cheaply.

Zuma Tower is a repeatable climbing event that uses **Zuma Visions** (the updated currency replacing Pearl of Finding). Each run begins at Floor 1 and continues until the player chooses to stop and claim rewards. Claiming rewards immediately ends the run and resets progress back to Floor 1.

Movement within the tower is determined by spending Zuma Visions on exploration attempts. Each attempt can:

- Move you up 1 or 2 floors
- Keep you on the same floor
- Drop you down 1 or 2 floors

Your **current floor at the moment you claim** determines the reward tier you receive. You do not earn rewards for every floor you pass, only for the final floor reached when claiming.

At its most basic level, Zuma Tower functions as a **currency conversion system**:

- **1 Zuma Vision ≈ 1 Stardust (minimum)**
- Worst-case outcome: spend 5 Zuma Visions → claim 5 Stardust

This establishes a hard floor value for the event. Even with extremely poor luck, players will not receive less than this baseline unless they overspend chasing higher floors. The cost of Safe Exploration rise quite significantly and as such it is generally not worth doing if you are after Stardust here. Climbing is for players who are after Castle skins.

- Farming **Crimsons**, do the first exploration then claim, repeat.
- If looking for **Stardust,**do the first exploration and then chance a free exploration – since levels 1 and 2 both holds 5 stardust you have nothing to lose! Claim 5 stardust or a bonus if you are lucky on the free attempt.

## Legendary Battle Pass — Relevant Later, Not a Season 1 Priority

Once you reach **Castle 40**, the Legendary Battle Pass becomes a different proposition. At that point your account direction is normally much clearer, and progression toward Holy, Shadow and eventually Chaos-related development starts becoming more relevant.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/image-206-1024x561.png)

For mid and higher spenders, the pass can offer good value because you are now buying resources that connect much more directly to your longer-term account plan.

For smaller spenders, however, there is no need to treat it as mandatory. If you are not yet developing the immortals or systems that benefit from those rewards, spending elsewhere can be more efficient.

This is one of the broader lessons of spending in Infinity Kingdom: **a good-value item is not automatically good value for your account right now**.

---

## Conclusion

Season 1 spending should be about building an account rather than buying a Season 1 march.

That distinction becomes increasingly important as Infinity Kingdom's event and shop systems change. The old Stardust Wheel priority is gone, Divine Well is now a much more expensive weekly proposition, and Cinders no longer fit neatly into the old Cinder Wish Shop recommendation.

The safest purchases are therefore the ones that remain useful regardless of where your account eventually goes. **Golden Path, sensible daily progression and taking advantage of GTArcade cumulative rewards and vouchers** are difficult to regret. More specialized spending should come after you understand what you actually intend to play.

For most light and mid spenders, spending a little less in Season 1 and keeping that budget available for Conquest is often better than trying to buy every early advantage.

Season 1 is temporary. Your account is not.
