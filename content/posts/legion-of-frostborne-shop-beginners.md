## Legion’s Vender for Beginners: Which Ability Stones Should You Buy?

Legion’s Vender is one of the most important long-term shops in Infinity Kingdom, but it is also one of the easiest places for a new player to make an expensive mistake. The shop uses Cold Silver, often simply called Cold Coins by players, and contains exclusive Ability Stones that can unlock some of the strongest Tower of Knowledge skills in the game.

The mistake many beginners make is treating Legion’s Vender like a normal event shop. They earn some Cold Silver, see a skill available and begin buying stones because they assume the currency should be spent before the shop closes. What the game does not explain very clearly is that the selection expands as your server progresses. This continues throughout Conquest, which is divided into five phases, and the complete shop is not available until Conquest P5.

Cold Silver can be carried forward. If none of the currently available skills belongs in your planned march, you can simply keep the currency and use it during a later season.

For newer players, that may be the most important thing to understand about the entire shop.

## How Much Does a Complete Skill Cost?

Every skill requires 60 Ability Stones to unlock, and each stone costs 800 Cold Silver.

### 60 Ability Stones × 800 Cold Silver = 48,000 Cold Silver

A player who reaches the highest contribution milestones and receives strong alliance and objective rewards can potentially earn enough for approximately one complete skill from a very successful KvK. Many beginners will earn less and need to combine their Cold Silver from two or more seasons.

This means Ability Stones are not small purchases. Buying 20 stones costs 16,000 Cold Silver, while buying 40 costs 32,000. If those stones belong to a skill you never use, you may have delayed a genuinely important skill by an entire KvK season.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4-1024x529.png)

Keeping Cold Silver in its original form gives you complete flexibility. Once it has been converted into a particular Ability Stone, it is tied to that skill unless you later use the dismantling and Cinder system.

## Legion’s Vender Expands with Your Server

The shop you see during your first Legion of Frostborne is not the final version. New Ability Stones are added as your server moves through Season 1, Season 2 and the five Conquest phases.

Patch 2.8.3 merged the old Seasons 2 and 3, so modern servers now move through the following structure:

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-1.png)

The important point is that entering Conquest does not immediately unlock everything. Conquest itself contains five phases, and the shop continues developing as you progress through them. By P5, the full Ability Stone selection is available:

- Assembly Charge
- Abundant Spirit
- Snipe
- Unforeseen Attack
- Stormeye
- Spell Disintegrator
- Wind Rage
- Berserker
- Weakness Protection
- Victorious Pursuit
- Siren’s Gaze
- Full Coverage

Unlocking all twelve would cost a total of:

### 12 skills × 48,000 Cold Silver = 576,000 Cold Silver

Even with maximum or near-maximum KvK rewards, that represents roughly eleven complete payouts. Under normal conditions, most players are looking at twelve or more strong KvK seasons before the collection is finished.

You therefore cannot treat every skill as equally urgent. Eventually, it is worth collecting the full shop, but your first purchases should be the skills that provide the greatest lasting improvement to your account.

## The Three Skills You Should Plan Around

Among the complete selection, three skills stand out as top-tier long-term targets: **Wind Rage, Assembly Charge and Abundant Spirit**.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-5.png)

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-6.png)

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-7.png)

They do not all belong in the same type of march, but between them they cover the two most important offensive directions: physical normal-attack builds and triggered magic builds. A newer player should know about these skills well before they actually become available, because they can change whether spending Cold Silver early is sensible.

### Wind Rage

Wind Rage gives every normal attack a chance to trigger an additional normal attack. That makes it far more than a basic attack-speed skill, because each extra attack can interact with other normal-attack mechanics.

It works particularly well with physical carries such as Alexander and Hannibal, as well as skills including Combo, Cleave and Assembly Charge. The extra attacks increase direct damage while giving the Immortal more opportunities to activate other triggered effects.

For an Alexander player, Wind Rage is normally a much more important long-term purchase than Snipe or Stormeye. If it is not available in your current shop, banking Cold Silver until the appropriate Conquest phase is often the correct decision.

Wind Rage is one of the safest physical investments in Legion’s Vender because its mechanic remains useful anywhere normal attacks form a significant part of the march’s damage.

### Assembly Charge

Assembly Charge is another top-tier physical skill, especially for fast-attacking Immortals. Every normal attack has a chance to deal physical damage to the target and two nearby enemies while also increasing the user’s Physical Damage.

The skill becomes increasingly reliable as the Immortal attacks more frequently. A slow attacker receives fewer opportunities to trigger it, while an Alexander build using attack-speed effects and Wind Rage can activate it repeatedly throughout the battle.

This is why Wind Rage and Assembly Charge form such a natural pairing. Wind Rage produces additional normal attacks, and every additional attack creates another opportunity for Assembly Charge to trigger. One skill feeds directly into the other.

If you are planning a physical normal-attack carry, these two skills should sit near the top of your long-term Cold Silver plan. Earlier skills may still be worth unlocking, but you should avoid reaching the later Conquest phases without enough currency to secure these when they become available.

### Abundant Spirit

Abundant Spirit is the corresponding top-tier target for mage builds. It increases Magic Damage while also improving the trigger chance of triggered Tower of Knowledge skills.

That makes it especially valuable on mages using several triggered damage skills. Spell Disintegrator, Annihilation, Fire Nova and similar effects become more consistent, while the permanent Magic Damage increase also improves the rest of the Immortal’s magic-based output.

Abundant Spirit works so well because it improves more than one part of the build at once. It strengthens magic damage directly and makes the triggered skills around it activate more often. On Immortals such as Wu, Himiko, Baldwin and other serious mage carries, it can become one of the strongest offensive skills available.

Mage players should still consider Spell Disintegrator before Abundant Spirit becomes available. The two are not competing for the same role and work extremely well together. Spell Disintegrator provides repeated area damage and Magic Defense reduction, while Abundant Spirit increases Magic Damage and helps Spell Disintegrator trigger more consistently.

## How I Would Prioritise Skills by Build

A useful priority list must begin with the march rather than the shop. Wind Rage may be top tier, but that does not mean it should be the first purchase for a Merlin main. Abundant Spirit may be one of the strongest magic skills in the game, but Alexander has no reason to equip it.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-2.png)

This is why a universal ranking can be misleading. Wind Rage, Assembly Charge and Abundant Spirit are the standout long-term skills, but they support different types of damage dealer.

For most physical normal-attack accounts, Wind Rage and Assembly Charge are the main project. For mage accounts, Spell Disintegrator and Abundant Spirit form the corresponding core.

## What About the Other Skills?

The remaining Legion’s Vender skills are not necessarily bad. They are simply more dependent on a particular Immortal, mechanic or team structure.

**Snipe** can be a very good early skill for physical backline carries. It targets the enemy positioned furthest away, which gives physical marches a way to apply pressure directly to the opposing backline. If you use a carry such as Khan, Yoshitsune or Gilgamesh and expect Snipe to remain in the build, it can be worth completing before the later Conquest skills arrive.

**Spell Disintegrator** remains one of the strongest general magic purchases. It deals repeated area magic damage and reduces enemy Magic Defense, allowing the rest of your magic damage to hit harder. Mage players should not skip it simply because Abundant Spirit becomes available later; the two skills complement one another.

**Stormeye** deals magic damage and gains additional value against Rooted enemies. It can perform well in the correct setup, but part of its potential is tied to a condition. For a new mage player without reliable Root, Spell Disintegrator and Abundant Spirit are generally simply much better and safer investments.

**Unforeseen Attack** provides another form of pressure against the enemy backline. Its value depends on whether your build actually wants focused backline targeting, so it should be purchased as part of a deliberate setup rather than because it appears later in Conquest.

**Berserker** can be powerful in attack-speed builds, particularly when its drawbacks do not interfere with the Immortal’s ults (i.e. Alexander and Hannibal). It is useful in the right setup but is not as universally safe as Wind Rage or Assembly Charge.

**Victorious Pursuit** requires a build capable of producing enough damage instances to stack its effect efficiently. It can become very strong, but most beginners will not yet have the skills and Immortals needed to exploit it properly. Excellent for Manco-builds.

**Siren’s Gaze** provides control through its Taunt and Silence interactions. It belongs in specialized tank and disruption builds rather than being a standard first purchase. A skill I personally like early to mid game for its ability to setup control builds well. That being said, it will never be top tier.

**Full Coverage**, can be excellent specifically for Fire builds on Hippolyta.

**Weakness Protection** is a defensive mitigation skill that has never really found its place in the meta.

Many of the above skills are only mostly interesting as you begin building secondary and third marches. They should normally come after the skills that directly establish your main march’s damage engine.

## Why Banking Cold Silver Matters So Much

Imagine that you are building Alexander and finish Season 1 with 22,000 Cold Silver. Snipe is available, but you do not have a permanent use for it. Buying 27 Snipe stones would give the feeling of progress, but those stones would not improve your active march.

If you keep the 22,000 instead and earn another 24,000 during the next KvK, you enter the following shop with 46,000 Cold Silver. A small amount from a later reward is then enough to complete a full 48,000-cost skill.

The same logic continues inside Conquest. Perhaps you already have Spell Disintegrator and none of the current P2 or P3 additions matters to your main march. There is no reason to spend the balance on a weaker option when Assembly Charge or Abundant Spirit will appear during a later phase.

This is what makes Cold Silver different from many ordinary event currencies. Its value is not tied only to the current shop. It can be carried forward until the skill you actually need is added.

For a physical player, that may mean holding enough to buy Wind Rage and then immediately beginning the next 48,000 reserve for Assembly Charge. For a mage player, it may mean completing Spell Disintegrator during an earlier season and then refusing every unnecessary purchase until Abundant Spirit becomes available.

## Is It Safe to Buy Partial Stones?

There is nothing wrong with buying a skill gradually when you are certain that you want it. Purchasing 25 Wind Rage stones now and the remaining 35 during the next KvK gives the same final skill as saving enough to buy all 60 at once.

The problem arises when players buy partial stones without having made a long-term decision. Owning 20 Snipe stones, 15 Stormeye stones and 18 Berserker stones leaves you with three unfinished skills and no usable upgrade.

Raw Cold Silver can still become any skill offered during a future phase. Partial Ability Stones are less flexible, even though the dismantling and Cinder systems provide ways to repurpose them later.

My general rule is simple: if you know you will complete the skill, buying partial stones is fine. If you are still comparing options, keep the Cold Silver.

The permanent purchase limit is 60 stones for each skill. Screenshots may show a lower remaining limit or display a skill as sold out when stones have already been purchased, but the full initial allowance is enough to unlock the complete skill.

## Using Unwanted Stones for Cinders

Ability Stones can be dismantled into Cinders, which can then be used to acquire other skills through the Wisdom Well Cinder Shop. This gives KvK stones value even after you have obtained the most important skills from Legion’s Vender.

There is also a Buyback Shop for Ability Stones previously purchased and dismantled. This protects players who recycle a skill and later decide that they need it after all, although the account-wide repurchase limits still need to be respected.

For beginners, this should be treated as a later layer of the system. Do not buy a poor-fitting skill simply because you might recycle it one day. Secure Wind Rage, Assembly Charge, Abundant Spirit and whichever other skills directly support your marches first. Once your important Legion’s Vender collection is in place, Cold Silver can become another route into the wider Cinder economy.

## Final Recommendation

Legion’s Vender is a progression shop, not a one-season shopping list. The selection expands through Season 1, Season 2 and all five phases of Conquest, with the complete 12-skill selection available at P5.

New players should plan for that complete shop from the beginning. If you are building a physical normal-attack carry, make sure you can obtain Wind Rage and Assembly Charge as soon as they become available. If you are building around a mage, Spell Disintegrator is an excellent earlier purchase, while Abundant Spirit should become a major target later in the progression.

Skills such as Snipe, Stormeye and Unforeseen Attack can still be good purchases when they serve a specific build. What you should avoid is buying them automatically because they happen to be available before the skill you really want.

Cold Silver does not become less valuable while sitting in your inventory. In many cases, the patient player who spends nothing during an early shop will reach Conquest with a much stronger skill collection than the player who bought a little of everything.

## TL;DR

- One complete skill costs **48,000 Cold Silver**: 60 stones at 800 each.
- Conquest contains **five phases**, and the shop continues expanding throughout them.
- The complete **12-skill selection** becomes available at **Conquest P5**.
- Unlocking all twelve costs **576,000 Cold Silver**, so purchases must be prioritised.
- **Wind Rage** and **Assembly Charge** are top-tier targets for physical normal-attack builds.
- **Abundant Spirit** is a top-tier target for serious mage and triggered-skill builds.
- **Spell Disintegrator** remains an excellent earlier purchase for mage players.
- Cold Silver can be banked across seasons and Conquest phases.
- Partial stones are fine when you are committed to completing the skill.
- When you are uncertain, keep the Cold Silver and wait for the later shop.
