Chapter 5 of the *Infinity Kingdom Lore Series* continues exactly where we left Brynhild and the Hangover Knight last time. After escaping the Arena and disappearing deep beneath Vitas, the two have ended up somewhere they very clearly weren't supposed to find.

The official title this week is **“Secrets of the Floating Towel,”** accompanied by the following description:

> “The Philosopher's Stone has appeared, and the hunt continues.”

The Floating Towel part is admittedly still a bit of a mystery to me, but the second part immediately caught my attention. The **Philosopher's Stone** is one of the oldest and most recognizable items in *Infinity Kingdom*. We've been using them to summon Immortals since the beginning of the game, but there has never been much reason to stop and think about what these stones actually are within the world of Norheim.

[**Chapter 5 may finally be taking us in that direction.**](https://www.facebook.com/reel/4818199961740759)

## What Is Hiding Beneath Vitas?

At the end of Chapter 4, Brynhild led our Hangover Knight through the passages beneath Vitas while the pair were escaping from the Arena. That escape eventually went badly wrong, and both of them fell an enormous distance into whatever lies far below the city.

We speculated at the time that they might have reached the Abyss mentioned in Chapter 3, or perhaps some kind of ancient dragon den after the episode briefly showed us a blue and a green dragon below them. Chapter 5 doesn't immediately give us a name for where they have landed, but we do get a much better look at what is down there.

Brynhild and our hero find what appears to be some kind of facility or laboratory, filled with huge containers holding the spirits or souls of dead warriors. It is one of the darker things we've seen in the lore series so far, particularly when we learn that these warriors aren't simply dead. They are apparently being used... Our Hangover Knight explains that the dead here aren't being allowed to rest and that **they are being used as fuel**.

Whatever is happening beneath Vitas, somebody has found a way to capture the dead and put their souls to work.

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-137-1024x469.png)

## Brynhild and Valhalla

Brynhild's reaction to all of this is particularly interesting. When she sees the dead warriors, she questions why they are here rather than in **Valhalla**. It's a relatively small line, but I really like what it tells us about the way the lore series is handling the Immortals.

Brynhild isn't simply being used as a recognizable *Infinity Kingdom* character. The writers are also bringing her mythology with her. Brynhild comes from Norse and Germanic heroic tradition and is closely associated with the Valkyries, so her immediate concern about fallen warriors being denied their journey to Valhalla makes perfect sense for the character.

It also makes what is happening beneath the city considerably worse from her perspective. These aren't merely bodies being experimented upon. Warriors who should have passed on after death are somehow being held here instead, their souls prevented from reaching where Brynhild believes they belong. Exactly who is responsible becomes one of the bigger questions of the episode.

## “The Council Is Forging a False God”

Chapter 5 actually opens with what may turn out to be one of the most important lines we've heard in the series so far.

A dying man delivers a warning: **“Tell them the Council is forging a false god.”**

We aren't given enough information yet to fully understand what that means, but after seeing the facility beneath Vitas, it is difficult not to connect the two. Somebody is collecting the souls of dead warriors and using them as fuel. At the same time, we're being warned that a group called **the Council** is attempting to forge a “false god.” Those two things appearing in the same episode feels very deliberate.

We still don't know exactly who the Council are, what they are trying to create or whether the souls are directly connected to this false god. For now, I think it is better to leave those questions open. What we can say is that the story has suddenly become much bigger than the black-market adventure we started with in Chapter 3.

Something is happening beneath Norheim, and whatever it is seems to involve powers that go well beyond ordinary warfare.

## The Sanctuary and the Covenant with the Goddess

After escaping the facility, we get another interesting piece of Norheim history from our Hangover Knight. Their destination is apparently a place called **the Sanctuary**, which he describes as the place where humanity once made a **covenant with the Goddess**.

That immediately raises another collection of questions. Who exactly is the Goddess? What was humanity given in return for this covenant? What did humanity promise? And does any of this have something to do with the sacred emblem our hero has been carrying since Chapter 3? We don't know yet, but the Sanctuary is apparently important enough that Brynhild believes they may find clues there concerning the stone they are looking for.

This is where Chapter 5 connects the larger mystery with something every *Infinity Kingdom* player knows very well.

## The Philosopher's Stone

The official description specifically tells us that **the Philosopher's Stone has appeared**, and that is probably the biggest lore connection in Chapter 5 from a player's perspective.

Philosopher's Stones have been part of *Infinity Kingdom* for as long as I can remember. We collect them, take them to the Hall of Immortals and use them to summon Immortals. For most of us they are simply another resource, albeit a particularly important one.

But what does a Philosopher's Stone actually do in Norheim?

![](https://ik-wiki.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image-138-1024x496.png)

The gameplay itself has always given us a pretty enormous clue. Somehow, these stones allow us to summon legendary figures from throughout history and mythology into the world of *Infinity Kingdom*. If the lore series is now going to explain where they came from and why they have that power, it could finally put some actual story behind one of the game's oldest mechanics.

There is a slight complication here because the terminology isn't completely consistent. The official Facebook description calls it the **Philosopher's Stone**, while the episode's subtitles appear to use **Sorcerer's Stone**. That may simply be a translation difference, and given the official description and the very familiar item already existing in the game, the Philosopher's Stone connection certainly seems intentional.

For now, though, we don't know what Brynhild and the Hangover Knight expect to find or why the Sanctuary might contain information about it.

## The Pieces Are Starting to Connect

There are now quite a few mysteries running through this story, and Chapter 5 makes it increasingly difficult to view them as separate events.

Our Hangover Knight found the ancient sacred emblem in Vitas. The official description for Chapter 3 told us that its reappearance would begin a journey into the Abyss. He then reunited with Brynhild, and together they fell deep beneath Vitas, where we briefly saw Water and Wind Dragons.

Now they have discovered the souls of dead warriors being harvested as fuel. We hear that the Council is forging a false god. We learn about an ancient covenant between humanity and the Goddess. And somewhere within all of this is the Philosopher's Stone.

I don't think we have enough information yet to put all of those pieces together, but it certainly feels as though they belong to the same larger story.

It also makes me wonder whether the Philosopher's Stones we casually use every day are connected to that ancient covenant in some way. Perhaps their ability to summon Immortals isn't simply a gameplay abstraction after all.

That is speculation for now, but Chapter 5 is finally giving us enough information to start asking those questions.

## And What About Our Hangover Knight?

One mystery the episode does **not** solve is the identity of our protagonist.

After Chapter 4, we had an interesting clue. Brynhild, a Water Immortal, fell into the depths alongside our unnamed hero, after which the episode deliberately showed us two dragons: one blue for Water and one green for Wind.

That led us to wonder whether the Hangover Knight could himself be a **Wind Immortal**, with Arthur being one possible candidate.

Chapter 5 doesn't really give us anything substantial to confirm that theory, so I'm not ready to move it beyond speculation. If anything, I'm happy the series hasn't immediately revealed him. Trying to work out who this guy is has become part of the fun of following the story.

For now, he's still our Hangover Knight.

## My Take on Chapter 5

Chapter 5 is probably the most lore-heavy episode we've had since the beginning of the series, and there is a surprising amount packed into a very short video.

The underground facility and the trapped warriors are interesting on their own, but Brynhild's reference to Valhalla makes the scene much better. It shows that the historical and mythological identities of the Immortals aren't being discarded when they enter Norheim. Brynhild still sees the world through the mythology she comes from, and I hope we get much more of that as other Immortals appear.

The bigger Norheim story is becoming interesting as well. A Council forging a false god, souls being harvested as fuel, an ancient covenant with a Goddess and a Sanctuary that may hold information about the Philosopher's Stone is quite a jump from following a drunk knight through the black market two chapters ago.

Most of all, I'm interested in the Philosopher's Stone.

It's something we've all clicked on thousands of times without giving it much thought. If this series actually intends to explain why Philosopher's Stones can summon Immortals, where they originated and how they fit into the history of Norheim, that is exactly the kind of lore I wanted from these videos in the first place.

The episode ends with Brynhild and our Hangover Knight emerging from below only to find what appears to be a city or fortress burning in the distance. Whatever they discovered underground, the situation above ground hasn't exactly improved while they were gone.

So we're heading into Chapter 6 with quite a list of questions. What is the Council creating? What is the false god? What happened during humanity's covenant with the Goddess? What is hidden inside the Sanctuary? And what exactly is the Philosopher's Stone within the actual lore of *Infinity Kingdom*?

Hopefully we won't have to wait too long for some answers.
